What Makes a Northern Virginia to Connecticut Move Different?

Connecticut has several route-specific considerations that are especially important when moving household goods in a large box truck.

1. Connecticut parkway restrictions — essential for moving trucks

Do not route a moving truck onto Connecticut's restricted parkways.

Connecticut DOT explicitly prohibits box trucks on the Merritt Parkway, Wilbur Cross Parkway, and Milford Parkway. The parkways also have strict dimensional limits.

Merritt Parkway — No box trucks

Route 15, Greenwich to Milford

Wilbur Cross Parkway — No box trucks

Route 15, Milford to Meriden

Milford Parkway — No box trucks

SR 796, Milford

Connecticut's published parkway dimension limits prohibit vehicles exceeding any of the following:

Height: 8 feet
Length: 24 feet
Width: 7 feet 6 inches, excluding mirrors

These dimensional limits are additional to the restrictions on box trucks and other commercial vehicles.

Connecticut DOT — Official Parkway Restrictions →

For a 26-foot moving truck, this is especially important. Even if a passenger-car GPS suggests the Merritt Parkway as the fastest route, the moving truck must use a legally permitted alternative.

The complete route should be checked using the truck's actual dimensions, weight, and commercial-vehicle restrictions.

2. I-95, I-84, and I-91 route planning

Many coastal Connecticut destinations are reached using I-95 and appropriate local connections.

Inland destinations such as Danbury and Hartford may involve I-84, I-91, or other suitable highways.

The fastest passenger-car route is not necessarily suitable for a commercial moving truck.

Connecticut also permits local authorities to establish certain truck restrictions on municipal roads, so the final approach to a property should be checked rather than assuming every road is accessible.

3. Parking and loading arrangements

Parking requirements vary by municipality and street.

Stamford, Greenwich, Norwalk, New Haven, and Hartford do not share one statewide moving-truck parking permit.

Before your move, confirm whether the truck can legally stop at the destination and whether a temporary parking or street-use authorization is needed.

For apartment buildings, ask whether management provides a designated loading area.

4. Historic homes and narrow access

Some Connecticut properties have older staircases, narrow hallways, tight entrances, or limited driveway space.

Measure sectional sofas, bed frames, dining tables, and oversized furniture before moving day.

Share photographs of difficult access points so the moving requirements can be evaluated.

5. Winter weather

Connecticut can experience snow, freezing rain, and icy roads during winter.

If you're moving between late fall and early spring, check forecasts, road conditions, and the accessibility of driveways and walkways.

Severe weather may affect transportation or delivery arrangements. Review your moving agreement for scheduling and communication procedures.

6. Coastal weather and flooding

Some Connecticut destinations are near Long Island Sound.

Coastal storms, heavy rain, and flooding can affect certain roads and waterfront properties.

Check conditions before moving day and confirm whether your destination has any temporary access restrictions.

7. Apartment and condominium requirements

Buildings in Stamford, New Haven, Hartford, and other cities may require freight elevator reservations, loading-dock access, certificates of insurance, designated moving hours, or move-in deposits.

These requirements are property-specific.

Ask your building manager for written instructions and share them with Haul Bros before scheduling.

Are my belongings protected during an interstate move?
