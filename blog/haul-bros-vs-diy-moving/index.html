What a Rental Truck Actually Costs (Beyond the Daily Rate)

The number on the side of the truck is never the real total. A typical U-Haul-style daily rate of $20–$70 doesn't include:

  • **Mileage fees** — roughly $0.59–$1.99 per mile on local rentals
  • **Fuel** — a fully loaded 26-foot truck gets poor gas mileage; expect $100–$300+ depending on distance
  • **Damage waiver / supplemental liability** — $10–$18 per day if you don't already have coverage through insurance or a credit card
  • **Equipment** — dollies, furniture pads, and straps are often extra rental fees
  • **Lost wages** — a day (or two) off work to load, drive, and unload

Once these are added, a local DIY move commonly lands at $150–$300 for a small apartment, but can climb toward $1,000 for a larger home — and a cross-country DIY move often runs $1,800–$5,300 in truck rental alone before fuel is even added.

The Real Costs DIY Movers Often Forget

Time. A DIY local move takes most people 8–14 hours; a professional crew handles the same job in 3–5. That's a full day (or more) of your life you don't get back.

Physical risk. Moving heavy furniture without training is one of the most common ways people injure their backs. There's no line item for that on a rental agreement.

Damage liability. If you scrape the truck, drop a dresser down the stairs, or crack a TV screen, that cost is entirely yours. Movers carry liability coverage as standard.

Driving an unfamiliar vehicle. A 15–26 foot truck handles nothing like your car — tight turns, low clearances, and wide blind spots all add risk, especially on unfamiliar routes for long-distance moves.

Multiple trips or a second day. Underestimate your inventory or your timeline, and a "one-day" DIY move can become two, doubling your rental cost.

When DIY Actually Makes Sense

DIY moving isn't a bad option — it just fits a narrower set of situations than people expect:

  • A small, short-distance move (studio or 1-bedroom, under an hour's drive)
  • You have reliable help for loading and unloading
  • You're comfortable driving a large rental truck
  • You have flexible time and no risk of a second trip
  • Your belongings are mostly low-value or easily replaced

For everything else — larger homes, stairs, long distances, tight timelines, or anything fragile or valuable — the bundled, insured price of professional movers usually closes the cost gap fast, and sometimes comes out ahead once your time and risk are counted.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is DIY moving actually cheaper than hiring movers?

For small, short local moves, often yes — a rental truck can total $150–$300. But once fuel, mileage, equipment, damage risk, and your own time are factored in, the gap narrows significantly, and for larger or long-distance moves, professional movers can end up costing about the same or less per hour of effort saved.

How much does a DIY local move cost in 2026?

Typically $118–$1,073 depending on truck size, distance, and rental duration, before accounting for fuel, tolls, or lost work time.

How much does a DIY long-distance move cost?

Cross-country truck rentals commonly run $1,800–$5,300 before fuel, compared to $4,300–$7,200+ for the same move with full-service movers — a much smaller gap than most people expect.

Does a rental truck come with insurance for my belongings?

No. Rental companies offer optional damage waivers for the truck itself, but your household items typically aren't covered unless you buy separate insurance. Professional movers like Haul Bros carry liability coverage as part of the service.

How long does a DIY move take compared to hiring movers?

A DIY local move commonly takes 8–14 hours; a professional crew typically finishes the same move in 3–5 hours.

What's included in a Haul Bros moving rate that isn't included in a truck rental?

Is it worth hiring movers for a small apartment move?

It depends on your budget and how much your time and physical effort are worth to you. For a studio or 1-bedroom with no stairs, DIY is often genuinely cheaper. For anything larger or with access challenges, movers frequently close the cost gap.
