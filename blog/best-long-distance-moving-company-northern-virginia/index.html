Quick Answer: How Do You Choose the Best Long-Distance Mover?

Choose a mover whose authorized services match your route, whose written estimate clearly covers your inventory, and whose pickup and delivery terms fit your schedule. Confirm whether you are dealing with a carrier or broker, review valuation protection, and ask how additional charges and claims are handled. A low advertised price alone is not enough to compare offers.

1. Start With Your Route and Moving Requirements

A move across state lines is generally an interstate move even when the distance is short. A longer move entirely within Virginia is not automatically interstate simply because it exceeds 100 miles. The shipment’s transportation arrangement matters; ask which rules and authority apply to your specific route.

3. Verify the Carrier and Understand Brokers

A carrier transports the shipment. A broker arranges transportation with a carrier. Ask who will load, transport, and deliver your goods, and whether another company will take part.

Use FMCSA’s official moving resources to check an interstate mover or broker. A USDOT number identifies a registration record; it does not alone establish authority for every service. Match the legal business name and authorized service to the company on your documents.

Haul Bros Moving Co LLC lists USDOT 4497497. Confirm the current record and the actual carrier arrangement for your shipment before signing. This guide does not represent an independent verification of current interstate operating authority.

5. Understand Your Written Estimate

Ask whether the estimate is binding or nonbinding, how it was prepared, and which inventory and services it covers. Understand what happens if your inventory, access, or requested services change.

Review packing, storage, shuttle service, long carries, extra stops, and valuation charges where applicable. Never sign blank or incomplete documents. Keep copies of the estimate, inventory, order for service, bill of lading, and payment records.

FMCSA recommends comparing written estimates and verifying credentials when selecting an interstate mover. For a move wholly within Virginia, confirm the applicable state requirements instead of assuming interstate rules apply.

6. Confirm Pickup Dates and Delivery Windows

Distance alone does not determine a delivery date. Routing, shipment arrangements, loading schedules, weather, access, and other operational factors can affect timing.

Ask whether transportation is dedicated or shared, whether goods may be transferred, and what delivery window appears in the agreement. Obtain any guaranteed dates and related terms in writing. Do not assume same-day delivery or an exclusive truck unless confirmed.

Keep medications, identification, essential clothing, chargers, valuables, and necessary work items with you. Plan temporary accommodation around the agreed delivery window.

7. Valuation Protection Is Different From Insurance

For interstate household-goods moves, FMCSA describes Full Value Protection and Released Value as two levels of mover liability. Full Value Protection generally provides a repair, replacement, or settlement remedy under the applicable terms; review its cost, deductible, and limitations.

Released Value limits the mover’s liability to 60 cents per pound per article. For example, a 20-pound item would have a $12 liability limit under that option, regardless of its purchase price. This is not replacement-cost protection.

Separate third-party insurance is different from mover valuation. Review high-value declarations, owner-packed items, exclusions, and the claims procedure before selecting coverage. Describe valuable and specialty belongings during the inventory review.

8. Red Flags When Comparing Moving Companies

  • A price offered without a meaningful inventory review.
  • Pressure to sign immediately or pay a large deposit without clear written terms.
  • Unclear carrier identity or inconsistent business names.
  • Refusal to explain credentials, valuation, or delivery arrangements.
  • Blank paperwork or undocumented promises.
  • A quote that omits packing, access, or storage requirements you already disclosed.

A single review or low quote should not decide the move. Compare the documents, service details, and recent customer experiences together.

9. Packing and Property Access

Decide what you will pack and what the crew will pack. Explore our packing services. Label boxes by room, identify fragile items, and keep an inventory of valuable belongings. Ask which materials and packing services appear in the estimate.

Reserve elevators and loading docks at both properties. Confirm legal truck parking, driveway clearance, gate access, and any certificate-of-insurance requirements. Share access changes promptly so the moving plan can be reviewed.

11. A Simple Plan for Requesting a Quote

  1. Send your pickup and delivery addresses, preferred date, and household inventory.
  2. Discuss packing, access, specialty items, and delivery needs with the moving coordinator.
  3. Review the written estimate, carrier details, protection options, and reservation terms.
  4. Confirm availability and the agreed services before making the reservation.

Your inquiry starts an estimate request. It does not automatically reserve a truck or date. Keep the final written agreement and your coordinator’s contact information accessible throughout the move.

12. Questions to Ask Before Booking

  • Are you the transporting carrier or a broker for this shipment?
  • Which company will perform each part of the move?
  • What authority applies to this route?
  • What inventory and services does the estimate cover?
  • What could change the final price?
  • What pickup and delivery dates are agreed?
  • Will belongings be transferred or stored?
  • What valuation options, exclusions, and claim procedures apply?
  • What are the deposit, cancellation, and rescheduling terms?
  • Who should I contact while the shipment is in transit?

13. Frequently Asked Questions

Who is the best long-distance mover in Northern Virginia?

The best fit depends on your route, inventory, schedule, and service needs. Compare authorized services, written estimates, delivery arrangements, and current customer feedback. Haul Bros is an option to discuss without claiming an independent number-one ranking.

Is every move over 100 miles an interstate move?

No. Mileage alone does not determine interstate status. Confirm the shipment’s route and applicable transportation rules.

Can I use local hourly rates for an interstate move?

Do not assume local packages apply. Obtain a route-specific written estimate covering transportation and requested services.

How long does delivery take?

Delivery depends on the agreed transportation arrangement and route. Request a written delivery window rather than relying on a general mileage estimate.

Are valuation and insurance the same?

No. Mover valuation describes liability for loss or damage under the moving agreement. Third-party insurance is a separate product with its own terms.

Can I request packing services?

Ask for full or partial packing in your estimate and identify fragile or high-value items. Materials and specialist needs should be specified.

Can I book a dedicated truck?

Ask whether dedicated transportation is available for your route and date, and have any agreed exclusivity documented. Do not infer it from a photograph.
