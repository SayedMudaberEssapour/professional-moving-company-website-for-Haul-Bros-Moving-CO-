What’s Included in Professional Moving Services? A Complete Guide

By Haul Bros Moving Co LLC · September 24, 2026

Hiring professional movers? Learn what a standard moving service typically includes, which services may cost extra, and what to confirm before booking your Northern Virginia move.

Hiring a professional moving company should make moving day easier. But before you book, there's an important question to ask:

What exactly is included in the price?

Does the moving company provide the truck? Will the crew wrap your furniture? Do movers disassemble beds and put them back together? Are boxes, packing services, stairs, and fuel included—or will they appear as additional charges?

The answers vary depending on the company, type of move, and services listed in your agreement.

Whether you're moving from an apartment in Woodbridge to a townhouse in Fairfax, relocating within Alexandria, or moving your family from Manassas to Stafford, understanding your moving estimate can help you avoid misunderstandings and compare companies fairly.

This guide explains what professional movers typically provide, which services may cost extra, what movers generally cannot transport, and how to prepare for moving day.

Quick answer: What is included in professional moving services?

A standard full-service local move commonly includes a moving crew, loading, transportation in a moving truck, and unloading at the destination. Furniture protection, basic disassembly and reassembly, equipment, and furniture placement may also be included, depending on the company and written agreement.

Packing your belongings into boxes, supplying all packing materials, unpacking, storage, specialty-item handling, and junk removal are not automatically included.

Before booking, request a written estimate that identifies the services, equipment, labor, transportation, minimum charges, and any applicable additional fees.

2. Professional movers and trained moving labor

One of the main things you're paying for is the moving crew.

Professional movers handle the physical work involved in transporting household belongings, including carrying furniture, maneuvering items through doorways, loading the truck, and unloading at the destination.

A crew may also perform agreed furniture protection, disassembly, reassembly, and placement.

How many movers are included?

That depends on your estimate.

A smaller apartment may be assigned two movers, while a larger home or a move involving multiple flights of stairs may require three or four movers—or a custom staffing plan.

More movers can sometimes shorten the job, but they do not automatically produce a lower total cost.

Ask: “How many movers are included in my estimate, and what happens if the move takes longer than expected?”

3. Is the moving truck included?

A moving truck is commonly included when you book a crew-and-truck moving package.

However, some companies advertise labor-only rates, while others may list truck use or transportation separately.

Confirm that your estimate includes:

  • The moving truck and its planned capacity.
  • Transportation between your pickup and delivery addresses.
  • Any applicable fuel, mileage, toll, or travel charges.
  • Additional trips if the shipment does not fit.

A truck's length alone does not guarantee that it can hold a particular home size. Capacity depends on the actual volume and shape of your belongings.

4. Are loading and unloading included?

For a standard move that includes transportation, loading at the pickup property and unloading at the destination are normally core services.

The crew carries the agreed items, organizes and secures the load for transportation, and unloads them at your new property.

However, the exact scope matters.

For example, your estimate should clarify whether movers are collecting items from a basement, garage, attic, storage unit, or additional address.

If you have two pickup locations or need an extra stop, disclose that before booking.

Will movers put furniture in the correct rooms?

Many movers place furniture and boxes in the rooms you identify during unloading.

Tell the crew where items belong and clearly label boxes.

If you want furniture rearranged repeatedly after initial placement, ask whether that additional work is included in the agreed service.

5. Do professional movers provide furniture protection?

Furniture protection is an important part of preparing household belongings for transportation.

Depending on the company and agreement, movers may use moving blankets, stretch wrap, protective padding, and other materials.

Protection methods should be appropriate for the item.

A wooden dresser, upholstered sofa, glass tabletop, and delicate antique may require different preparation.

For example, a piano, heavy safe, or oversized appliance may require specialized handling or equipment.

Moving and packing are separate services unless your estimate explicitly combines them.

A standard move may assume that you've already packed loose belongings into sealed boxes.

Professional packing services may include preparing kitchenware, books, clothing, decorations, electronics, and other household items for transportation.

Packing can be arranged for the entire home or selected rooms, depending on the company.

9. Are moving boxes and packing supplies included?

14. What items will professional movers not transport?

Not everything in your home can be placed on a moving truck.

Restrictions vary by carrier, shipment type, applicable law, and the specific item.

Common categories that require special attention include hazardous materials, flammable liquids, explosives, certain chemicals, and items that could leak or create a fire risk.

Examples that movers may prohibit include gasoline, propane cylinders, paint thinner, and certain other hazardous products.

For interstate household-goods moves, FMCSA provides consumer guidance on hazardous materials and items that should not be packed in the moving shipment.

Other items may require special arrangements, including:

  • Live animals.
  • Perishable food.
  • Plants, particularly when crossing state lines.
  • Prescription medication.
  • Cash, jewelry, important documents, and other valuables.
  • Firearms or other regulated items, where applicable.
  • Extremely heavy or unusually large equipment.

Not every item in this list is universally prohibited. Some should simply be transported personally, while others may be accepted under specific procedures.

Before moving day, request your mover's actual prohibited-items list.

Keep medications, identification, keys, financial documents, and irreplaceable valuables with you.

15. Are specialty items included in standard moving services?

A moving company's standard rate may not cover every item.

Some belongings require additional labor, equipment, protective materials, or specialized expertise.

Examples include upright and grand pianos, large safes, oversized refrigerators, treadmills, pool tables, heavy exercise equipment, and unusually large mirrors.

Do not assume that because an item fits through a doorway, it can safely be handled by the assigned crew.

Provide photographs, measurements, estimated weight, and details about stairs or access.

The company should confirm whether it can handle the item and include the agreed service and price in writing.

16. What protection do professional movers provide if something is damaged?

Furniture wrapping and moving blankets are physical protection measures. They are not the same thing as insurance or a mover's legal liability for loss and damage.

For interstate household-goods moves, federal regulations require movers to offer customers a choice between Full Value Protection and Released Value liability.

Released Value provides limited liability of $0.60 per pound per article, while Full Value Protection provides a different level of responsibility under applicable terms and exceptions. FMCSA explains these options in its consumer guidance.

For moves entirely within Virginia, the applicable liability terms and consumer protections should be reviewed under the company's intrastate tariff, agreement, and relevant Virginia requirements. Do not assume federal interstate valuation rules apply identically.

17. Is moving insurance included?

The word insurance is often used loosely in moving discussions, so it's important to distinguish different types of coverage.

A moving company's commercial auto or general liability insurance is not automatically the same as protection for the full replacement value of your household belongings.

Likewise, a mover's valuation or liability option should not automatically be described as an insurance policy.

Ask the company to explain its actual liability terms and any optional protection available for your move.

For interstate moves, FMCSA's consumer guidance explains the distinction between Full Value Protection and Released Value.

If you are considering separate third-party moving insurance, review its terms and exclusions directly with the insurer.

19. What does a professional moving company do on moving day?

A standard local move generally follows a predictable sequence.

1. Arrival and walkthrough

The crew confirms the items being moved, reviews access, and identifies belongings needing special handling.

2. Furniture preparation

Movers use agreed protective materials and perform necessary basic disassembly when included.

3. Loading

The team carries the agreed belongings to the truck and secures them for transportation.

4. Transportation

The truck travels to the delivery address according to the agreed moving plan.

5. Unloading and furniture placement

Movers unload the shipment and place belongings in the designated rooms under the agreed scope.

6. Reassembly and final walkthrough

The crew completes agreed reassembly and reviews the finished move and paperwork with the customer.

If you've purchased packing, unpacking, storage, or other services, the sequence may include additional steps or separate appointments.

21. How to compare moving quotes fairly

Imagine you're comparing two companies for a two-bedroom move from Woodbridge to Fairfax.

One advertises a lower hourly rate, but its quote excludes truck use, fuel, and furniture protection.

The other provides a higher combined crew-and-truck rate that includes those services.

You cannot tell which estimate is less expensive until you compare the total cost for the same inventory and scope.

Ask each mover to provide the following information in writing:

  • Crew size, hourly or fixed price, and minimum hours.
  • Truck and transportation charges.
  • Included equipment and protective materials.
  • Furniture disassembly and reassembly.
  • Packing labor and materials.
  • Access-related charges and billing policies.
  • Specialty-item charges.
  • Liability and valuation terms.
  • Deposit, cancellation, and payment terms.
  • Conditions that could change the final price.

For interstate moves, FMCSA recommends obtaining written estimates and comparing services rather than relying solely on the quoted price.

The goal is to compare equivalent services—not just advertised rates.
