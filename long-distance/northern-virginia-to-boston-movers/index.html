What Makes a Northern Virginia to Boston Move Different?

There are several Boston-specific details that can affect your moving experience. Planning them in advance can help prevent avoidable problems on delivery day.

1. Boston moving-truck parking permits

Boston offers moving-truck parking permits that reserve legal curbside parking for a specified moving day.

A permit is not universally required, but it can help secure a legal unloading location. The city's standard permit reserves two parking spaces from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Official Boston permit guidance

Moving-truck parking permit

TWO NON-METERED SPACES

$69

Published one-day permit price

TWO METERED SPACES

$109

Application timing: Boston's current permit FAQ says online applications are available when the moving date is 15 days to eight weeks away. In-person applications are available when the move is at least three days away and no more than eight weeks away.

Sign posting: The city requires permit signs to be posted at least 48 hours before the reserved period. If you're moving from Virginia, arrange for someone local to receive and post the signs.

Official Boston Parking Permit Guide →

The permit must apply to an eligible parking location. State-owned roads and certain event-affected streets may have different rules, so confirm the exact address before applying.

2. Boston's low bridges and truck-restricted roads

This is one of the most important route-specific safety considerations.

Massachusetts officials warn that Storrow Drive and Soldiers Field Road in Boston, as well as Memorial Drive in Cambridge, are restricted to cars and have bridge clearances as low as nine feet in some locations. Large moving trucks must not use these roads.

Your moving truck's overall height, length, weight, and permitted route must be checked before dispatch.

Do not rely solely on a passenger-car navigation route when driving a box truck.

3. Tunnel and bridge clearances

Some Greater Boston routes involve tunnels or other height-restricted infrastructure.

MassDOT provides a truck-height checking resource with clearance information for selected roads and tunnels. Use the truck's actual measured height and verify the complete route before travel.

This is particularly important for a large box truck with a tall cargo body.

4. Historic buildings and narrow staircases

Boston's older residential buildings can have narrow doorways, steep staircases, small landings, and limited elevator access.

Before moving day, measure oversized furniture and identify any difficult turns or entrances.

Photographs and measurements help determine whether furniture disassembly or additional handling may be necessary.

5. September apartment move-ins

Boston experiences substantial apartment turnover around September 1, especially in student-heavy neighborhoods.

If your move falls near the beginning of September, coordinate your lease dates, building access, parking arrangements, and moving schedule as early as possible.

The Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation specifically issues moving-season warnings about low bridges and truck restrictions around this period.

6. Longer-distance delivery scheduling

Boston is farther from Northern Virginia than Philadelphia or many New Jersey destinations.

The overall schedule must account for loading, driving, applicable driver-hours requirements, traffic, rest, and unloading.

Do not assume that pickup and delivery will occur on the same day unless your moving agreement explicitly confirms that arrangement.

Do I need a parking permit for a moving truck in Boston?

A moving-truck parking permit is not universally required, but Boston offers permits that reserve legal curbside parking.

A standard one-day permit reserves two parking spaces from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. The city's published price is $69 for two non-metered spaces or $109 for two metered spaces.

Review the official Boston moving-truck permit instructions before applying.

How far in advance should I apply for a Boston moving permit?

Boston's current guidance allows online applications when the moving date is 15 days to eight weeks away. In-person applications are available when the move is at least three days away and no more than eight weeks away.

Permit signs must be posted at least 48 hours before the reserved period.

If you're moving from Virginia, arrange for someone in Boston to receive and post the signs if necessary.

Can a 26-foot moving truck drive on Storrow Drive?

No. Massachusetts officials state that Storrow Drive and Soldiers Field Road in Boston, and Memorial Drive in Cambridge, are restricted to cars and are unsuitable for large moving trucks.

Some bridge clearances are as low as nine feet. Drivers must use appropriate alternative routes.

Can a 26-foot moving truck drive through Boston's tunnels?

Do Boston apartment buildings require a certificate of insurance?

Some apartment and condominium buildings require a certificate of insurance before allowing a moving company onto the property.

Are my belongings protected during an interstate move?

Federal regulations require interstate household-goods movers to offer Full Value Protection and Released Value liability options.
