1. Why Is Apartment Moving Different From House Moving?

Moving from an apartment can involve more coordination than a move from a ground-level house.

Many apartment communities have rules regarding:

  • Moving hours.
  • Service elevator reservations.
  • Loading dock access.
  • Moving truck parking.
  • Building protection.
  • Certificates of insurance.
  • Move-in and move-out scheduling.
  • Hallway access.
  • Security and concierge check-in.

For example, a two-bedroom apartment move from the sixth floor of an Alexandria building may take longer than a similar move from a ground-floor apartment in Woodbridge.

Even if the amount of furniture is identical, elevator waiting times and carrying distances can affect the total.

The key to a successful apartment move is coordinating building access before moving day.

3. Elevator Reservations: One of the Most Important Apartment Moving Requirements

If your apartment building has an elevator, ask the property management team whether you need to reserve it for moving.

Some buildings require reservations for service elevators.

Others permit moving only during designated hours.

Questions to Ask Your Apartment Management

  1. Do I need to reserve a service elevator?
  2. What moving hours are permitted?
  3. Is there a moving deposit?
  4. Does the building require a certificate of insurance?
  5. Is there a designated loading dock?
  6. Can the moving truck park near the entrance?
  7. Does the building require elevator padding?
  8. Are there restrictions on weekends or holidays?
  9. Is there a required move-in or move-out inspection?
  10. What happens if the move exceeds the reservation window?

Why Elevator Reservations Matter

Imagine that you reserve an elevator from 9 AM to 1 PM.

If your movers arrive at 11 AM, the available moving window may be too short for the inventory.

Likewise, if the elevator is unavailable when the crew arrives, the team may experience delays.

Helpful tip: Share the elevator reservation details with your moving company before the move so the arrival window and crew plan can be coordinated.

8. Does Apartment Moving Cost More When There Are Stairs?

Stairs can increase the amount of labor required for a move.

A third-floor walk-up apartment may require movers to carry furniture and boxes up or down several flights of stairs.

Haul Bros Moving Co LLC does not charge a separate standard stair fee for qualifying local moves.

However, the additional carrying time can affect the total bill.

Example

Two customers have similar one-bedroom apartments.

Customer A: Ground-floor pickup and delivery.

Customer B: Third-floor walk-up pickup and second-floor walk-up delivery.

Customer B's move may require more time even though both customers have similar inventories.

This is why the number of floors and access details should be included when requesting an estimate.

9. Moving Truck Parking: What Apartment Residents Should Know

Parking is another important factor in apartment moving.

Some apartment buildings have designated loading zones.

Others require moving trucks to park on nearby streets.

Before Moving Day

Confirm:

  • Whether a loading dock is available.
  • Whether the truck can access the building entrance.
  • Whether parking reservations or permits are required.
  • Whether there are garage height restrictions.
  • Whether a moving truck can remain parked for the expected duration.
  • Whether there are restrictions on truck size.

Why Parking Distance Matters

If a moving truck must park far from the apartment entrance, movers may need to carry furniture and boxes a greater distance.

That can increase the amount of time required for loading and unloading.

Important Safety Reminder

Do not assume a moving truck can enter a parking garage.

Truck height and clearance requirements must be checked before attempting entry.

11. How to Prepare Your Apartment Before the Movers Arrive

Preparation can help reduce delays and make moving day more organized.

Two to Four Weeks Before Moving

  • Confirm your moving date.
  • Request moving estimates.
  • Reserve the elevator if required.
  • Check building moving rules.
  • Arrange parking.
  • Begin decluttering.
  • Order packing materials.
  • Schedule utilities and internet.

One Week Before Moving

  • Pack nonessential belongings.
  • Label boxes by room.
  • Confirm the furniture inventory.
  • Arrange furniture disassembly if needed.
  • Identify fragile or specialty items.
  • Confirm the moving crew's arrival window.

The Day Before Moving

  • Finish packing.
  • Clear hallways.
  • Prepare appliances.
  • Separate valuables and important documents.
  • Confirm building access.
  • Keep pets away from work areas.

Moving Day

  • Meet the moving crew.
  • Review the inventory.
  • Confirm elevator and parking access.
  • Identify fragile items.
  • Direct furniture placement at the destination.
  • Complete a final walkthrough.
