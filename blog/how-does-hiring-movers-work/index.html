How Does Hiring Movers Actually Work? A First-Timer's Guide — Understanding the Basic Process of Hiring Movers (2026)

By the Haul Bros Moving team, Woodbridge, VA Updated September 20, 2026 8 minute read

If you have never hired movers, the process can feel murky: quotes that do not match, fees you did not expect, and paperwork you are not sure you should sign. It is simpler than it looks. The seven steps of hiring movers

  1. Decide what kind of help you need

    Movers offer different levels of service, and knowing which one you need keeps quotes comparable.

    • Full-service local move. A crew and truck load, drive and unload for you. Our local moves include a 26-foot truck with dollies and straps, furniture blankets, shrink wrap and floor protection.
    • Labor-only help. A crew loads or unloads a rental truck, a PODS container or a storage unit that you arrange yourself.
    • Packing and unpacking. Full-home, partial or fragile-only. See our packing services.
    • Extras. Junk removal, storage coordination and apartment or commercial moves.

    Distance matters too. In Virginia, moves of 30 miles or less and moves beyond 30 miles fall under different state operating authority, and longer moves are typically priced by weight and distance instead of by the hour. Our guide to local vs. long-distance moving costs explains the difference.

  2. Get written quotes from at least three movers

    Give every company the same details: both addresses, your date, a rough inventory, and access at each end, including stairs, elevators, the walk from truck to door, and parking. For a larger home, ask for a video walkthrough or an in-home estimate.

    Then compare the same things in each quote: crew size, hourly rate, minimum time, any trip or travel fee, materials, and what protection is included.

    How estimates work. For an hourly local move, an estimate is a projection of how long the job will take, and the final bill follows the actual time. Ask each mover whether its price is binding or only an estimate. We ask customers to confirm pricing on a current written estimate. See our rates below.

  3. Verify each mover before you book

    • Virginia DMV. The DMV regulates household goods movers operating within the state. Look for the company in the DMV's database of authorized motor carriers and ask for its authorization number. A mover that cannot give you one is a red flag.
    • Insurance and bonding. Household goods carriers must keep a $50,000 surety bond or letter of credit on file with the DMV. Ask for a certificate of insurance and ask what happens if something is damaged.
    • Federal registration. Interstate movers must be registered with FMCSA and have a USDOT number. FMCSA's Protect Your Move site has a lookup tool.
    • Reviews. Read recent reviews on several sites and look for patterns, such as surprise charges or late arrivals, rather than a single bad day.

    Haul Bros operates under US DOT #4497497, carries cargo and liability insurance, and is rated 5.0 across 183 Google reviews. Check any mover, including us, before you book.

  4. Book your date and read the paperwork

    Book four to six weeks ahead when you can. Summer, weekends and the last days of the month fill first.

    Before you sign or pay anything, get these in writing: the rate and any minimum, what is included, deposit and cancellation terms, and how damage claims are handled. A reputable mover has a written cancellation policy and explains it plainly. Never sign a contract with blank fields. If something is damaged, our claims page explains how to report it.

  5. Prepare your home

    With hourly billing, every minute the crew spends waiting on boxes or clearing a hallway is a minute you pay for. A short checklist saves real money:

    • Pack and label boxes by room, and keep them to a weight one person can lift.
    • Keep valuables, documents, medications and chargers with you instead of on the truck.
    • Empty the refrigerator and freezer, and take fragile, liquid and valuable items out of drawers. Ask your mover about dressers with light clothing.
    • Clear pathways, stairs and doorways.
    • At an apartment or condo, reserve the elevator and a loading spot, and ask your property manager about move-in rules or a certificate of insurance requirement.
    • Plan where kids and pets will be on moving day.

    We handle standard furniture disassembly and reassembly, so beds and tables do not need to be taken apart in advance. Need packing tips? Read our how-to-pack guide.

  6. Meet the crew on moving day

    Someone should be there to greet the crew, walk them through what is moving, and point out fragile or high-value items. Here is how a Haul Bros local move runs:

    • The crew arrives with a 26-foot truck, dollies and straps.
    • Furniture is wrapped in blankets and shrink wrap, and floors are protected.
    • Standard beds and tables are taken apart as needed, with hardware kept together with each piece.
    • Everything is loaded, driven to your new address and unloaded.
    • Furniture and boxes are placed in the rooms you choose.

  7. Walk through, pay and follow up

    Before you sign off, walk every room, closet and the garage with the crew lead. Check furniture for damage, confirm nothing was left behind, and photograph and note any problem before you sign.

    Compare the final invoice with your written estimate. Payment terms vary by mover and move type. Local moves are commonly settled once the work is finished, but confirm how and when you will pay before moving day. Tipping is optional; many customers tip for a hard job or great service.

Red flags when hiring movers

  • No DMV authorization or USDOT number, or a number that does not match the company name on the truck and paperwork.
  • A quote given with no inventory, video walkthrough or questions about access.
  • A large cash deposit demanded upfront.
  • An unmarked rental truck arriving on moving day.
  • A contract with blank fields, or pressure to sign right away.
  • Vague answers about who will do your move, what insurance covers, or how claims work.

Frequently asked questions

How does hiring movers work?

You get written quotes from a few movers, verify each company, book a date, prepare your home, meet the crew on moving day, do a final walkthrough, and pay. Most local moves are billed by the hour, so preparation lowers your cost.

How far in advance should I book movers?

Booking four to six weeks ahead is a common recommendation. Book earlier for summer, weekends and the last days of the month, when demand is highest. Call us at 571-899-1919 to check availability for your date.

Do I have to be home while the movers work?

Someone you trust should be there at least at the start and end of the move to point out fragile items, answer questions and do the final walkthrough.

What is the difference between hourly and flat-rate moving prices?

With hourly pricing you pay for the time on the clock, so preparation lowers your bill. A flat rate is a fixed price for a defined job and is more common on longer moves priced by weight and distance. Ask any mover whether its quote is binding or only an estimate.

How do I know a mover is licensed in Virginia?

Search for the company in the Virginia DMV's database of authorized motor carriers and ask for its authorization number. Interstate movers must also be registered with FMCSA and have a USDOT number. Haul Bros operates under US DOT #4497497.

Should I tip my movers?

Tipping is optional. Many customers tip for a difficult move or especially good service.
