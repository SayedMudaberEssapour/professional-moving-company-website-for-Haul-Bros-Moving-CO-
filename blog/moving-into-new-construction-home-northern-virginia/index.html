Updated October 2026 | 2027 New Home Relocation Guide

Moving into a brand-new home is an exciting milestone. Whether you've purchased a newly built townhouse in Woodbridge, a spacious single-family home in Stafford, or a new construction property in Gainesville, Haymarket, or Manassas, the experience represents a fresh start.

But moving into a new construction home is different from moving into an existing property.

Your closing date may change. Contractors may still be completing final work. The driveway or landscaping may not be finished. New hardwood floors, freshly painted walls, cabinets, and stair railings need protection during move-in.

You may also need to coordinate with the builder, settlement company, homeowners association, utility providers, and moving company.

At Haul Bros Moving Co LLC, we provide residential moving, packing, furniture handling, and labor-only services throughout Northern Virginia.

This complete 2027 guide explains what to do before closing, how to protect your new home, what moving services cost, and how to prepare for a successful move-in.

Quick Answer: How Do You Move Into a New Construction Home?

The most important step is to confirm that your home is legally ready for occupancy and that you have possession before scheduling the final move-in.

Here are seven essential steps:

  1. Confirm the builder's completion schedule and expected closing date.
  2. Complete your final walkthrough and document outstanding issues.
  3. Verify the required certificate of occupancy and possession arrangements.
  4. Confirm electricity, water, heating, and other essential utilities.
  5. Measure doorways, staircases, and large furniture.
  6. Arrange safe moving truck access and floor protection.
  7. Book a professional moving crew with an accurate inventory and a plan for possible closing delays.

1. Why Moving Into a Newly Built Home Is Different

A new construction move involves two overlapping processes: completing the home purchase and physically relocating your belongings.

Unlike an existing home, a newly built property may still be undergoing inspections, finishing work, landscaping, or neighborhood construction.

Common New Construction Moving Challenges

Changing Closing Dates

Construction timelines can shift because of inspections, weather, materials, or other issues.

Incomplete Exterior Work

Sidewalks, driveways, lawns, or nearby roads may still be under construction.

Protecting New Finishes

Freshly installed flooring, painted walls, trim, cabinets, and staircases require careful handling.

Builder Access Restrictions

The builder may have specific rules about moving in before possession.

Limited Truck Parking

New developments may have construction vehicles, narrow roads, or temporary parking restrictions.

Large Furniture

Newly built homes can have stairways, entryways, or room layouts that require furniture measurements.

Utility Coordination

New addresses and utility connections sometimes require additional verification.

A detailed plan helps avoid unnecessary delays and unexpected moving expenses.

3. Confirm Your Closing Date Before Booking Movers

One of the biggest mistakes new construction buyers make is scheduling movers based solely on the builder's estimated completion date.

An estimated completion date is not the same as a guaranteed closing date.

What Can Delay a New Construction Closing?

  • Final inspections.
  • Unfinished construction.
  • Permit or occupancy documentation.
  • Weather.
  • Material delivery delays.
  • Financing or settlement issues.
  • Title-related issues.
  • Outstanding contractual requirements.

Best Scheduling Strategy

Begin requesting moving estimates four to six weeks before the anticipated closing.

Tell your moving company that the destination is a new construction property.

Confirm the final moving appointment once the builder and settlement professionals have provided reliable information about closing and possession.

If your closing date remains uncertain, discuss the company's availability and rescheduling terms before paying a deposit.

Should You Move on Closing Day?

Moving on closing day can be risky.

Settlement appointments may be delayed, access may not be immediately available, and the property may need a final walkthrough.

If possible, schedule the move after you have confirmed possession and access.

4. What Is a Certificate of Occupancy, and Why Does It Matter?

A certificate of occupancy, commonly called a CO, is an important part of the new construction process.

Virginia's building code generally requires a certificate of occupancy before a newly constructed building can be occupied, subject to applicable provisions and exceptions.

A building official may also issue a temporary certificate of occupancy when the relevant conditions are satisfied.

Before Moving In, Ask Your Builder:

  • Has the required certificate of occupancy been issued?
  • Is it final or temporary?
  • Are there any occupancy restrictions?
  • Have the required inspections been completed?
  • Is the home approved for residential use?
  • When will possession be delivered?
  • Are contractors still scheduled to work inside?

Do not assume a house is ready for move-in simply because it appears finished.

Your builder, settlement agent, or appropriate local building department can help clarify the home's status.

5. Complete Your Final Builder Walkthrough

Before moving furniture into your new home, complete the builder's walkthrough and document any outstanding concerns.

This is sometimes called a blue-tape walkthrough or punch-list inspection.

It is not a substitute for an independent professional home inspection.

Interior Walkthrough Checklist

Walls and Paint

Look for scratches, dents, unfinished areas, and paint defects.

Flooring

Check for scratches, chips, loose flooring, and visible installation issues.

Doors

Open and close interior and exterior doors.

Windows

Check operation, locks, and visible damage.

Cabinets

Inspect doors, drawers, hardware, and visible finishes.

Countertops

Check for chips, cracks, and other visible issues.

Bathrooms

Review fixtures, visible leaks, and drainage.

Kitchen

Inspect installed appliances and finishes.

Electrical

Test accessible switches and outlets where appropriate.

Heating and Cooling

Ask the builder to demonstrate installed systems and controls.

Staircases

Inspect railings, trim, and visible finishes.

Exterior Walkthrough Checklist

  • Driveway condition.
  • Walkways.
  • Garage doors.
  • Exterior lighting.
  • Drainage.
  • Landscaping.
  • Exterior steps.
  • Access points.

Document Everything

Take dated photographs and keep a written list of concerns.

Ask the builder how outstanding work will be addressed and when repairs are expected.

6. Protecting New Floors, Walls, and Staircases During Moving

A newly constructed home may have flooring, paint, trim, and other finishes that have never experienced a full household move.

Appropriate protection is important.

Hardwood Floors

Use suitable floor coverings and keep pathways clear.

Avoid dragging furniture across finished surfaces.

Luxury Vinyl Plank Flooring

Use protection appropriate for the manufacturer's recommendations.

Avoid adhesives or materials that could damage the finish.

Carpet

Protect frequently used pathways from dirt and moisture.

Tile

Take care when transporting heavy furniture across tiled surfaces.

Staircases

Stairs can be especially vulnerable to impacts from large furniture.

Discuss furniture dimensions and handling procedures before moving day.

Walls and Door Frames

Oversized furniture should be measured before attempting to move it through tight openings.

What Does Haul Bros Provide?

Haul Bros includes furniture blankets, shrink wrap for suitable furniture, routine moving equipment, and basic floor protection in qualifying standard local moving packages.

Additional protection needs should be discussed during the estimate.

7. Is a 26-Foot Moving Truck Enough for a New Construction Home?

Many customers moving into newly built homes are relocating from apartments, townhouses, or smaller single-family houses.

A 26-foot moving truck can be suitable for many residential moves, but capacity depends on the actual inventory and weight.

Consider the Following:

  • Number of bedrooms.
  • Number of beds.
  • Sectional sofas.
  • Dining furniture.
  • Office furniture.
  • Garage equipment.
  • Appliances.
  • Moving boxes.
  • Outdoor furniture.
  • Storage contents.

A larger household may require multiple truckloads, additional vehicles, or a different moving plan.

Why an Inventory Review Matters

A pre-move inspection can help determine:

  • Appropriate crew size.
  • Truck requirements.
  • Estimated duration.
  • Specialty equipment.
  • Packing needs.
  • Potential access challenges.

Do not assume one truck can accommodate a four- or five-bedroom household without reviewing the belongings.

10. Moving Into a New Construction Townhouse

New construction townhouses are a common consideration for buyers who want additional living space without purchasing a detached home.

However, townhouses often have multiple floors.

Common Moving Challenges

  • Narrow staircases.
  • Three-level floor plans.
  • Garage-level entrances.
  • Large sectional sofas.
  • King-size mattresses.
  • Heavy bedroom furniture.
  • Limited street parking.

Measure Furniture Before Moving

Check the dimensions of:

  • Sofas.
  • Beds.
  • Dressers.
  • Dining tables.
  • Entertainment centers.
  • Desks.
  • Exercise equipment.

Confirm Parking Rules

Some townhouse developments have HOA restrictions or limited guest parking.

Ask where the moving truck can legally park.

Plan Furniture Placement

Assign major furniture to rooms before the crew arrives.

This reduces unnecessary handling and helps keep the move organized.

12. What If Your New Construction Closing Gets Delayed?

Closing delays are one of the biggest scheduling risks for new construction buyers.

What Should You Do?

  1. Contact your builder or settlement agent.
  2. Confirm the revised expected possession date.
  3. Notify your moving company immediately.
  4. Review the reservation and rescheduling terms.
  5. Ask whether a new moving date is available.
  6. Consider temporary storage if your current housing arrangement ends first.

Can You Reschedule Your Move?

Rescheduling depends on the company's availability and the applicable reservation agreement.

Haul Bros customers should review the current deposit, cancellation, and rescheduling terms before booking.

Should You Book a Backup Date?

Discuss possible alternatives with your moving coordinator, but do not assume a second date is reserved unless explicitly confirmed.

13. New Construction Moving Checklist: Eight Weeks Before Move-In

Eight Weeks Before

  • Confirm your anticipated completion date.
  • Research moving companies.
  • Begin decluttering.
  • Create a furniture inventory.
  • Review the floor plan.
  • Identify specialty items.

Six Weeks Before

  • Request a moving estimate.
  • Schedule a virtual or in-home inventory review.
  • Discuss closing-date flexibility.
  • Begin packing nonessential belongings.
  • Review builder and HOA requirements.

Four Weeks Before

  • Confirm the anticipated walkthrough date.
  • Begin utility coordination.
  • Measure furniture and doorways.
  • Plan furniture placement.
  • Arrange professional packing assistance if needed.

Two Weeks Before

  • Review closing and possession details.
  • Confirm moving truck access.
  • Organize boxes by room.
  • Prepare a floor-protection plan.
  • Check the status of the certificate of occupancy.

One Week Before

  • Complete the builder walkthrough as scheduled.
  • Document outstanding concerns.
  • Confirm utility activation.
  • Verify possession arrangements.
  • Reconfirm the moving appointment.

Moving Day

  • Confirm access to the home.
  • Review truck parking.
  • Protect flooring and entryways.
  • Direct furniture placement.
  • Keep children and pets away from active moving areas.
  • Inspect the property after unloading.

First Week After Moving

  • Unpack essential rooms.
  • Inspect belongings and furniture.
  • Review the builder's remaining punch-list work.
  • Save warranty documents.
  • Update your mailing address.
  • Learn the community's trash and recycling procedures.
  • Review HOA rules and amenities.

14. Common Mistakes When Moving Into New Construction

Scheduling Movers Before Possession Is Confirmed

Avoid committing to a final move-in plan without understanding the builder's timeline.

Forgetting About the Certificate of Occupancy

Confirm the property's legal occupancy status.

Failing to Protect New Flooring

Discuss floor protection before moving day.

Assuming the Driveway Is Finished

Verify that the truck can safely access the property.

Forgetting HOA Rules

Some communities have parking or move-in restrictions.

Not Measuring Large Furniture

A large sofa may not fit through a narrow stairwell.

Ignoring Unfinished Construction Nearby

Construction vehicles and materials can affect access.

Underestimating the Inventory

Include garages, basements, closets, and outdoor furniture.

Forgetting Utilities

Confirm that the home has working electricity, water, and appropriate heating or cooling.

Not Documenting Builder Issues

Record visible defects and follow the builder's documented reporting procedures.
