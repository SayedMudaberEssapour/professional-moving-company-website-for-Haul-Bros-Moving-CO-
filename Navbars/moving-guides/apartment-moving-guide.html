How to Prepare for Apartment Moving Day

The complete Northern Virginia guide to building rules, elevators, parking, packing, and a smoother move.

Moving out of an apartment is different from moving out of a house. Elevators must be reserved, loading areas can be limited, hallway walls need protection, and property managers may enforce specific hours. In Northern Virginia—especially in Arlington, Alexandria, Tysons, Fairfax, Reston, and Woodbridge—the difference between an orderly move and a stressful one usually comes down to preparation.

In this guide

  1. Confirm building requirements
  2. Plan elevator and truck access
  3. Finish packing before arrival
  4. Prepare furniture and appliances
  5. Follow a moving-day plan
  6. Complete a final walkthrough

1. Confirm Your Building’s Moving Requirements

Contact both the building you are leaving and the building you are entering. Ask the leasing office or property manager whether moves must be scheduled in advance, which entrances movers may use, and whether there are restricted moving hours.

Some apartment communities require a certificate of insurance before allowing a moving company onto the property. If one is required, request the exact certificate holder name, address, coverage requirements, and submission email. Send that information to your mover early enough for the document to be prepared.

Questions to ask each building

  • Must the freight elevator be reserved?
  • Is there a move-in or move-out deposit?
  • Which door, loading dock, or service entrance should the crew use?
  • Are movers required to protect hallways, doors, or elevator walls?
  • Where can a 26-foot moving truck park?
  • Are there height restrictions, permits, or loading-time limits?
  • Does the building require proof of insurance?

2. Reserve the Elevator and Plan Truck Access

Elevator access is one of the biggest variables in apartment moving. Reserve the freight elevator for a window that begins before the movers arrive and ends after the expected loading or unloading period. If the building uses a key, fob, or elevator attendant, confirm who will provide access.

Walk the route from your apartment to the loading area. Note long corridors, tight turns, steps, ramps, low ceilings, and doors that require a code. Share those details when requesting your moving quote. A long carry between the truck and elevator can add substantial time even when the apartment itself is small.

In dense areas such as Arlington and Washington, D.C., curb access may require advance planning. Confirm whether the truck can use a loading zone or whether a temporary parking reservation or permit is needed. Do not assume an open curb will still be available on moving morning.

3. Complete Packing Before the Crew Arrives

Unless you booked professional packing services, all loose household items should be inside closed, labeled boxes before the arrival window. Partially packed rooms slow down loading and make it harder to keep related items together.

Use clear, useful labels

Write the destination room and a short description on the top and at least one side of each box. Mark fragile boxes clearly. For immediate-use items, label a box “OPEN FIRST” and carry it separately if possible.

Keep personal essentials with you

Do not pack medications, identification, keys, wallets, chargers, important documents, jewelry, or irreplaceable personal items on the moving truck. Prepare a personal bag with what you will need during the move and your first night.

Separate items movers cannot transport

Set aside hazardous, flammable, or leaking materials. Common examples include gasoline, propane, fireworks, certain chemicals, opened paint, and aerosol products exposed to extreme heat. Ask your moving company if you are unsure about a specific item.

4. Prepare Furniture, Electronics, and Appliances

Measure large furniture and compare those dimensions with doorways, elevator doors, stair turns, and the entrance at your new apartment. Tell your movers in advance about oversized sofas, solid-wood dressers, exercise equipment, safes, or other unusually heavy pieces so the crew arrives with the right tools and staffing.

Empty desks, dressers, bookcases, and cabinets unless your moving company tells you otherwise. Remove shelving pegs and loose glass shelves, then place small hardware in labeled bags. Photograph cable connections before unplugging electronics.

If your washer, dryer, refrigerator, or other appliance is moving, confirm who is responsible for disconnecting and reconnecting it. Water lines, gas lines, and hardwired appliances may require a qualified technician.

5. Use a Simple Moving-Day Plan

  1. Wake up early and finish any last personal packing before the arrival window.
  2. Keep your phone charged and remain reachable.
  3. Secure pets in a closed room or arrange for them to stay elsewhere.
  4. Meet the crew, explain building access, and point out fragile or high-priority pieces.
  5. Keep elevator keys, parking passes, and entry codes available.
  6. Do a room-by-room check before the truck leaves.
  7. At delivery, direct furniture and labeled boxes to their intended rooms.

Your moving time generally begins when the crew arrives and continues through unloading. Clear paths, packed boxes, reserved elevators, and immediate access help reduce avoidable delays. Review our moving rates and pricing when planning your budget.

6. Complete a Final Walkthrough

Before returning the keys, check closets, cabinets, balconies, storage cages, appliance interiors, and the area behind doors. Take timestamped photos of the cleaned, empty apartment and note any pre-existing building damage. At the new apartment, confirm that major furniture is placed correctly before the crew leaves.
