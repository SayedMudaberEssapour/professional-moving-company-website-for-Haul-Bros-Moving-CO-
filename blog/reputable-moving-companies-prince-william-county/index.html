Step 1: Check licensing and insurance

For moves within Virginia, the Virginia DMV generally requires Property Carrier authority for household-goods moves of 30 miles or less and Household Goods Carrier authority for longer moves, unless an exemption applies. A household goods carrier transporting only household goods does not need separate property authority for shorter moves.

Ask for evidence of applicable cargo and liability coverage, and confirm the shipment valuation and claim terms in your agreement. For interstate household moves, use FMCSA’s mover search to check registration and applicable operating authority. A USDOT number alone does not establish authority for every service.

Step 2: Get written estimates from two or three companies

Give each mover the same addresses, inventory, date, and access details. Compare:

  • Hourly versus flat-rate pricing and minimum hours.
  • Crew size, truck capacity, and when billing starts and stops.
  • Stairs, long carries, heavy items, parking, and travel charges.
  • Packing labor, materials, and furniture assembly.
  • Deposit, cancellation, and estimate-change terms.

Be cautious of a firm price offered without details about your belongings and access. A detailed inventory or appropriate walkthrough helps the mover understand the work.

Step 3: Read reviews on multiple platforms

Compare recent reviews on Google and other platforms such as Yelp, Angi, Birdeye, and the BBB. Look for patterns in communication, arrival, handling, and how final charges were explained. Read critical reviews and the company’s responses, too.

For Haul Bros and other movers, check original review pages for current ratings and counts. Individual experiences and platform ratings can change.

Step 4: Know the red flags

  • Unclear business identity or contact information.
  • A price far below other estimates without a clear explanation of scope.
  • A large upfront cash deposit or pressure to sign immediately.
  • No written agreement or inventory details.
  • Refusal to provide applicable credentials or insurance information.
  • Blank or vague paperwork.

Step 5: Know where to file a complaint

Keep your estimate, contract, messages, receipts, and photos of damage. Raise the issue with the mover in writing. For consumer complaint assistance, contact the Virginia Attorney General’s Consumer Protection Unit at 1-800-552-9963 from Virginia, or 804-786-2042 from Richmond or outside Virginia. The office can help identify the appropriate complaint channel. Operating-authority checks and consumer complaint resolution are separate processes.

Prince William County moving factors to plan for

  • I-95 and Route 1 traffic: Congestion can affect travel time. Discuss the route and billing for transportation.
  • HOA rules: Ask your community about moving hours and truck parking before moving day.
  • Townhomes and stairs: Report the number of floors, stair flights, and carrying distances when requesting an estimate.
  • Military moves: Share any firm deadlines or installation-access requirements and ask about discount eligibility.
  • Nearby communities: Confirm the rules with each property manager or local authority at both addresses, including moves involving Manassas or Manassas Park.
