9. Understand the difference between a moving carrier and a broker

When researching moving companies, ask who will actually perform the move.

A moving carrier transports your belongings. A moving broker arranges transportation through another company.

For interstate household-goods moves, FMCSA explains that both business types have registration requirements, but their responsibilities differ.

A customer booking through a broker should know which carrier will transport the shipment and review that carrier's information.

Ask these questions before booking:

“Will your own crew and truck perform my move?”

“If another company is involved, when will I receive its name and registration details?”

“Who will handle questions, delivery issues, or damage claims?”

Clear answers help you understand who is responsible for each part of the service.

12. Compare moving companies using the same information

Suppose you're planning a three-bedroom townhouse move from Manassas to Fairfax.

You contact three companies.

One estimates four hours with two movers. Another recommends six hours with three movers. The third provides a flat price without describing the crew or services.

These aren't directly comparable quotes yet.

To compare them fairly, give each company the same inventory and access details.

Then ask for the estimated total, crew size, expected duration, included services, and possible changes.

The lowest starting rate doesn't necessarily mean the lowest final bill.

Likewise, a higher estimate may reflect a larger crew, more complete service, or different assumptions.

The goal is to understand what each quote actually covers.

13. Red flags to investigate when hiring movers

Not every unclear estimate indicates misconduct, but certain issues deserve attention.

Investigate further if a company:

  • Refuses to provide its legal business name.
  • Cannot explain the authority applicable to your move.
  • Will not provide a written estimate.
  • Avoids explaining additional charges.
  • Gives a quote without asking about your inventory.
  • Pressures you to sign incomplete documents.
  • Cannot identify the company responsible for transporting your belongings.

FMCSA recommends researching interstate movers, reviewing complaint information, and obtaining written estimates before booking.

Take the time to resolve important questions before paying a deposit.
