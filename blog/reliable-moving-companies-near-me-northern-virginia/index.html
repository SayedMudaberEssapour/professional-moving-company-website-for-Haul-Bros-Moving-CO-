Step 2: Read reviews the smart way

Star ratings are only the start. Here is how to read reviews so they tell you something real:

Look for patterns, not single reviews. One glowing or angry review can be an outlier. Ten reviews mentioning the same strengths are a signal.

Check more than one site. Compare Google, Yelp, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Birdeye, and the Better Business Bureau.

Prefer specific reviews. A review that mentions the size of the move, the crew, and what went well is more useful than "Great service!"

Check recency. Reviews from the past few months show how the company performs now.

Read how the company responds to criticism.

Find reviews from moves like yours: apartment, large home, senior move, or last-minute move.

Step 3: Verify credentials before you book

Reviews tell you how a move feels. Credentials tell you whether the company is legitimate:

Operating authority: For interstate household moves, check FMCSA registration and applicable authority. For Virginia moves, check the appropriate Virginia DMV authority. A USDOT number alone does not establish authorization for every service.

Insurance: Ask for proof of liability and cargo coverage in writing.

Written estimate: It should list the hourly rate, minimum hours, crew size, truck size, and any extra fees.

Local address and phone: Call the number and confirm the office exists.

Broker or carrier: Ask whether the company does the move itself or subcontracts it. Confirm who is responsible for the work in your agreement.

Red flags when comparing movers

A quote far below every other estimate

No physical address

A large cash deposit demanded upfront

No written contract or inventory

Refusal to share a USDOT number or proof of insurance

Pressure to sign immediately

A review page with only vague five-star reviews and no detail
