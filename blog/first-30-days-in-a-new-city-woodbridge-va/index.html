Your First 30 Days in a New City: A Week-by-Week Guide (Woodbridge, VA)

By the Haul Bros Moving team, Woodbridge, VA Updated September 21, 2026 9 minute read

The truck has left, but moving is not quite finished. The first month in a new city is when boxes get unpacked, addresses get updated, and a place slowly turns into home. This guide splits the first 30 days into four weeks and adds the local details that matter if you are settling into Woodbridge or anywhere in Prince William County, Virginia.

The short answer

In your first 30 days in a new city, work in this order: make your home livable and get the essentials running in week one, learn your neighborhood in week two, build routines and meet people in week three, and start living like a local in week four. In Virginia, also watch two DMV clocks: 30 days to title and register your vehicle, and 60 days to get a Virginia driver's license.

Vehicle title and registration within 30 days. Virginia driver's license within 60 days.

Two Virginia deadlines to calendar on day one

Some moving tasks have no due date. These do, and both clocks start when you become a Virginia resident. Put them on your calendar before you unpack a single box.

The 30 days, week by week

  1. Week 1, days 1 to 7: make your home work

    Aim for comfort and the essentials, not a finished house.

    • Set up beds and the bathroom first, then a simple kitchen: dishes, a pan, coffee, and something easy to cook.
    • Confirm that power, water and internet all work. Use the cheat sheet above for who to call.
    • Sort out trash. Prince William County does not send trucks to your curb, so hire a private hauler (many HOAs arrange one), or take your own trash, recycling and yard waste to the county landfill or its Saturday collection sites. Glass goes in the purple recycling containers around the county, and apartment residents should ask their property manager. The county's A to Z Disposal Guide covers more than 400 items.
    • Check your belongings as you unpack. If something is damaged, photograph it and contact your mover right away. Our claims page explains how to report it.
    • Break down empty boxes. If you have bulky furniture you no longer want, our junk removal can haul it away.
    • Update your address with your employer, bank, insurers and the post office, and book your DMV visit now while appointments are easy to find.

    Tip. Finish one room completely before starting the next. A bedroom or kitchen that is fully set up gives you somewhere calm while the rest of the house is still in progress.

  2. Week 2, days 8 to 14: learn your neighborhood

    Get familiar with the ground within a few minutes of your door.

    • Test your commute at the real hour. I-95 and US Route 1 carry heavy commuter traffic. Woodbridge also has two Virginia Railway Express stations, Woodbridge and Rippon, and OmniRide buses connect here, so it is worth pricing out the train and bus before you settle into a driving routine.
    • Map your errands. Find your nearest grocery store, pharmacy, urgent care and gas station. For big shopping trips, Potomac Mills is the largest outlet mall in Virginia.
    • Get a library card. Prince William Public Libraries has 12 branches, and its programs, computers and Wi-Fi are free. Woodbridge has Potomac Library and Chinn Park Library.
    • Bookmark pwcva.gov. It is the county hub for parks, libraries, trash and recycling, permits and alerts.
    • Try the outdoors. Occoquan Bay National Wildlife Refuge sits where the Potomac River meets Occoquan Bay. Leesylvania State Park has hiking, fishing, boating and picnic areas on the Potomac (there is a per-car parking fee). The Neabsco Creek Boardwalk crosses about three-quarters of a mile of wetlands.
    • Go back to places you liked. A second visit to the same coffee shop or trail does more to make a place familiar than a fifth new one.

    If you have kids, let each one pick a park or restaurant for the weekend. Choosing a place gives them a stake in the new town.

    Tip. Write a short list of neighborhood go-tos: closest pharmacy, best takeout, favorite park, fastest route to work. It becomes your personal new-resident guide.

  3. Week 3, days 15 to 21: build routines and meet people

    Replace moving-week improvisation with a rhythm you can repeat.

    • Pick regular times for groceries, exercise, laundry and one weekend outing. Fewer daily decisions leave more energy for everything new.
    • Say hello to neighbors, and go to your apartment community or HOA events when they come up.
    • Volunteer. Prince William Public Libraries welcomes volunteers (call the Volunteer Office at 703-792-6100), and Virginia Cooperative Extension in Prince William lists local programs and volunteer opportunities.
    • Look for groups built around something you already enjoy, such as running, board games, books or gardening. Sites like Meetup and Nextdoor are a starting point.

    Friendships usually form through repetition, not one big night out. Choose one recurring thing and keep showing up.

  4. Week 4, days 22 to 30: start living like a resident

    Move from surviving the move to enjoying the place.

    • Spend a weekend in the Occoquan historic district, a small riverfront town of shops, restaurants and seasonal events, or tour Rippon Lodge, the oldest house in Prince William County (tours run through the summer).
    • Ask a coworker or neighbor where they go for an ordinary Tuesday dinner. Local recommendations beat any online list.
    • Check the DMV deadlines. Your 60-day license deadline lands soon after this month ends, so make sure your appointment is booked.
    • Do a five-minute review: What is still in boxes? Which errands have found their rhythm? Is the commute working? Did I talk with someone new this week? What do I want to try next month?

    Feeling at home is less about how many attractions you have visited and more about knowing your route, your go-to meal and your favorite place to take a walk.

What if it doesn't feel like home after a month?

That is normal. Thirty days builds a foundation, not a finish line. Settling in can take longer if you moved alone, changed jobs, face a tough commute, are helping children adjust, or live far from family and old friends. Keep your routines, keep in touch with people you miss, and give yourself another month.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do in my first 30 days in a new city?

Take it one week at a time: make your home livable and get essentials working in week one, learn the neighborhood in week two, build routines and meet people in week three, and start living like a local in week four. In Virginia, also calendar the DMV deadlines: 30 days to title and register your vehicle and 60 days to get a Virginia driver's license.

Does Prince William County pick up trash and recycling?

No. The county does not provide curbside trash or recycling collection. You can hire a private hauler, use a service arranged by your HOA, or take your own trash and recycling to the county landfill, the Saturday collection sites or recycling trailers. In an apartment or condo, ask your property manager.

How long do I have to get a Virginia driver's license after moving?

You have 60 days after moving to Virginia to get a Virginia driver's license. If you hold a commercial driver's license, the deadline is 30 days.

How long do I have to register my car after moving to Virginia?

Virginia requires you to title your vehicle within 30 days of moving here and register it, and you need insurance in place before you register. Check the Virginia DMV's New to Virginia page for current documents and steps.

Who provides utilities in Woodbridge, VA?

Water and sewer service comes from Prince William Water. Electricity comes from Dominion Energy or NOVEC, and natural gas from Washington Gas or Columbia Gas, depending on your address. Ask your landlord, HOA or listing agent which providers serve your home, and start service before you move in.

How can I commute from Woodbridge without driving the whole way?

Woodbridge has two Virginia Railway Express stations, Woodbridge and Rippon, on the Fredericksburg Line. OmniRide local and express buses also connect in Woodbridge.

What are good places to explore in Woodbridge as a newcomer?

Start with Occoquan Bay National Wildlife Refuge, Leesylvania State Park, the Neabsco Creek Boardwalk, Rippon Lodge and the Occoquan historic district, then add your local library and a few favorite restaurants to your routine.

How long does it take to feel at home in a new city?

There is no fixed timeline. It depends on your circumstances, and moving alone, changing jobs or relocating with children can take longer. Thirty days is a foundation, not a deadline.
