Average Moving Costs in Fairfax County, VA [2026 Guide]

Last updated: August 25, 2026

Whether you're moving from a Reston condo to a single-family home in Great Falls, or shifting between townhouses in Fairfax City, the cost of your move depends on a handful of predictable factors: how much you're moving, how far, and how easy your home is to access. Here's a real breakdown using Haul Bros' actual local rates, plus the extra costs Fairfax County movers commonly run into that aren't always mentioned upfront.

What Actually Changes Your Quote in Fairfax County

Building and neighborhood access

Fairfax County covers a wide range of housing types — high-rise condos in Tysons and Merrifield with loading dock reservations and certificate-of-insurance requirements, dense townhouse communities in Burke and Centreville with shared driveways and limited parking, and larger lots in Great Falls or Fairfax Station where a longer carry from the truck to the front door adds time. Each of these adds real minutes to a move, which is why an accurate estimate depends on describing your specific building or street, not just your square footage.

Stairs and elevators

Multi-level townhouses, common throughout Fairfax County, typically call for a 3-person crew rather than 2. Condo and apartment moves involving elevators should factor in reserving the elevator with building management in advance — a missed reservation window can delay your entire move.

Packing needs

Boxing your own kitchen and closets before movers arrive keeps your hours (and cost) down. Full or partial packing services add time and materials cost but can be worth it for fragile items, art, or when you're short on time before your moving date.

Timing

Fairfax County's moving demand follows the broader D.C.-area pattern: late May through September, weekends, and month-end dates are the busiest and should be booked 4–6 weeks ahead. Weekday and mid-month moves are easier to book on shorter notice and sometimes come with more schedule flexibility.

Distance beyond local

A move within Fairfax County or to a nearby jurisdiction (Arlington, Loudoun, Prince William) is typically priced hourly like the table above. A move outside the immediate D.C. region shifts to long-distance pricing based on weight and mileage rather than crew hours — ask for both if you're not sure which applies to you.

Ways to Lower Your Fairfax County Moving Cost

  • Declutter first. Donating or selling furniture and boxes you don't need before moving day directly reduces your inventory, your truck size, and your hours.
  • Move on a weekday. Tuesday–Thursday moves are typically easier to book and can come with more availability than weekend slots during peak season.
  • Pack yourself where you can. Boxing up closets, books, and kitchen items ahead of time shortens your crew's time on-site.
  • Confirm building requirements early. If you're moving into a high-rise in Tysons, Merrifield, or similar buildings, get your certificate of insurance and elevator reservation sorted before moving day — last-minute building holdups can turn into extra billed hours.
  • Get one quote based on your real inventory, not a generic size estimate — accurate information up front avoids a higher final bill from an underestimated crew or truck size.

Frequently Asked Questions About Moving Costs in Fairfax County, VA

How much does it cost to move a 2-bedroom apartment in Fairfax County?

A 2-bedroom typically needs a 3-mover crew, starting at $495 for the first two hours and $189 for each additional hour. Most 2-bedroom local moves run 3–5 hours, landing in the $700–$1,100 range depending on stairs, elevator access, and inventory.

Is $500 enough to move a 2-bedroom in Fairfax County?

It can cover the first two hours with a 3-mover crew, but most 2-bedroom moves take longer than two hours once loading, driving, and unloading are factored in, so $500 is a reasonable starting point, not a full budget. A written estimate based on your actual inventory gives a realistic total.

Why do movers charge by the hour instead of a flat rate?

Hourly pricing ties your cost to what your move actually takes rather than a generalized guess, so a fast, easy move costs less and a longer, harder one costs more. The trade-off is that access issues or extra inventory can extend the final hours if they weren't disclosed upfront.

What's a fair price for movers in Fairfax County, VA?

For context, local rates in the area typically start around $395–$695 for the first two hours depending on crew size, with $159–$249 per additional hour. Rates well below that range are worth double-checking for hidden fees, insurance coverage, and DOT licensing.

Do movers charge extra if my move takes longer than estimated?

Yes. Hourly moves bill for actual time worked, so if loading or access takes longer than expected, additional hours are billed at your rate tier. This is why disclosing stairs, long driveways, and full inventory upfront matters — it keeps the estimate close to the real total.

How much does it cost to move into a high-rise in Tysons?

Base crew rates are the same as any Fairfax County move, but high-rise moves in Tysons or Merrifield often run longer due to loading dock reservations and certificate-of-insurance requirements. Budget slightly more time than a similarly sized single-family move.

Do I need to tip movers in Fairfax County?

Tipping isn't required but is customary in the moving industry. Many customers tip $20–$40 per mover for a half-day local move, more for larger or physically demanding jobs, based on service quality.

How much does a last-minute move cost in Fairfax County?

Base hourly rates don't change for short-notice bookings, but availability does. Last-minute moves during peak season (late spring through summer, weekends, month-end) may have fewer crew slots open, so book as early as possible even for a small move.

Are there hidden fees I should watch out for with Fairfax County movers?

Ask specifically about charges for stairs, long carries, fuel, and travel time — some companies add these separately. Haul Bros' hourly rate already includes ordinary stairs, elevators, fuel, and the truck, with no added charge for those when disclosed at booking.

How do I get an accurate moving quote instead of a lowball estimate?

Give a full, honest inventory — every room, closet, and garage item — plus specific access details like driveway length, stairs, and parking. Vague or incomplete information is the number one reason a quote turns into a much higher final bill.
