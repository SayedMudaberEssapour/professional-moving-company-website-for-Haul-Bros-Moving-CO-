Best Moving Companies in Northern Virginia: How to Compare Movers and Choose the Right One

By Haul Bros Moving Co LLC · September 21, 2026

Looking for the best moving companies in Northern Virginia? Compare movers, reviews, pricing and services in Woodbridge, Fairfax, Alexandria and beyond.

Moving in Northern Virginia comes with its own challenges. A move from a Woodbridge townhouse might involve three flights of stairs. A move into an Arlington apartment could require a reserved freight elevator. A relocation from Fairfax to Loudoun County may involve a larger home, more furniture, and a longer drive.

With so many moving companies serving the region, how do you decide which one to contact?

The answer isn't simply to choose the lowest advertised hourly rate or the company with the largest number of reviews. The right moving company for your job is one that can legally perform the move, has experience with your type of property, provides a clear estimate, and explains how your belongings will be handled.

This guide introduces moving companies to research and explains how to compare them using consistent criteria.

2. How to identify a moving company that fits your needs

Start with the type of move

Before comparing companies, identify the service you're purchasing.

A one-bedroom apartment move in Arlington might require elevator coordination and a smaller crew. A four-bedroom house in Fairfax may require an inventory survey, more truck capacity, and additional movers.

A move from Virginia into Maryland or Washington, DC also requires different regulatory verification from a move entirely within Virginia.

Use your specific job to narrow the list instead of assuming every moving company offers the same services.

Verify operating authority before comparing prices

This should happen before paying a deposit.

Virginia DMV states that household-goods movers generally need the applicable intrastate authority unless exempt. For household-goods transportation of 30 miles or less from the starting point, the state describes Property Carrier authority; longer Virginia household-goods moves require Household Goods Carrier authority. A company with Household Goods Carrier authority does not need a separate Property Carrier authority solely for those shorter household-goods moves.

For interstate moves, FMCSA distinguishes being registered from being authorized to transport household goods. Search the federal mover database rather than assuming that a displayed USDOT number proves authorization.

Useful official resources:

  • Virginia DMV: Intrastate operating authority
  • FMCSA: Protect Your Move

A moving company's website is a useful starting point, but an official regulatory record is the appropriate place to verify authority.

3. How to compare customer reviews

Online reviews can reveal useful information about the customer experience, but an overall star rating doesn't tell the whole story.

Read recent reviews across more than one platform, and look for specific descriptions of the work performed.

For example, a review about moving a studio apartment may be less relevant to someone planning a large household move with fragile antiques.

Look for detailed accounts of:

  • Arrival and communication.
  • Furniture protection and handling.
  • Accuracy of the estimate.
  • Crew organization.
  • Treatment of stairs, elevators, and building rules.
  • Responsiveness when a customer reports a problem.

Also examine how the company responds to criticism. A complaint is one customer's account, not automatically proof of wrongdoing; a company's reply is likewise not independent verification that a dispute was resolved.

Avoid relying solely on the number of five-star reviews. Check recency, detail, platform, and relevance to your type of move.

4. How to compare moving quotes without getting surprised

Request estimates for the same inventory, pickup address, destination, and service scope.

A lower hourly rate doesn't necessarily produce a lower final bill.

For example, one company may include the truck and furniture protection in its quoted rate. Another may itemize transportation, materials, or travel separately.

Neither pricing structure tells you the total cost until you've added the applicable components.

8. How to prepare before requesting moving estimates

An accurate quote starts with accurate information.

Before contacting movers, gather your pickup and delivery addresses, preferred date, home size, furniture inventory, box estimate, and access details.

Include items in the garage, basement, attic, patio, and storage unit.

Photograph oversized furniture and identify anything unusually heavy or fragile.

If you're moving into an apartment, confirm the elevator reservation and loading rules.

If your move includes packing, tell the company which rooms need assistance.

A complete inventory reduces the chance of misunderstandings about the planned crew, truck capacity, and expected duration.

9. Red flags to investigate before hiring a moving company

One warning sign alone doesn't establish fraud, but certain practices justify further investigation.

Be cautious when a company will not provide its legal business name, refuses to explain its authority, avoids written estimates, or gives unclear answers about who will actually transport your belongings.

Other concerns include an estimate based on an incomplete inventory, unexplained changes to payment terms, or pressure to sign documents you haven't reviewed.

For interstate moves, FMCSA specifically advises consumers to understand whether they are dealing with a carrier or broker and to verify the business through its official resources.

A useful rule: If a company cannot clearly explain what it will do, what it will charge, and which business is responsible for your shipment, resolve those questions before booking.

Frequently Asked Questions About Moving Companies in Northern Virginia

1. What are the best moving companies in Northern Virginia?

Start with companies serving your exact route and compare verified authority, services, recent reviews, written estimates, and liability terms.

The appropriate choice depends on your household inventory, budget, schedule, and service requirements.

2. How do I find a reliable moving company near Woodbridge, VA?

Identify companies that serve Woodbridge, check their authority for your route, read recent reviews, and request detailed written estimates.

Tell each company whether you're moving from an apartment, townhouse, or house.

3. Are Northern Virginia moving companies required to be licensed?

Virginia generally requires applicable operating authority for for-hire intrastate household-goods transportation unless an exemption applies. The authority required depends in part on the distance and type of operation.

Verify the company's actual authority rather than relying only on advertising.

4. Is a USDOT number enough to prove a mover is authorized?

No. FMCSA explains that registration and authorization to transport household goods are different. For an interstate move, verify the company's current authority in the federal mover database.

5. How many moving quotes should I get?

Getting three comparable estimates is a practical starting point.

Use the same inventory and service requirements for each company so you can compare the totals fairly.

6. Are cheaper movers always a better deal?

No. The final cost depends on the pricing structure, time required, included services, and applicable additional charges.

Compare estimated totals and written terms rather than hourly rates alone.

7. How much do movers charge in Northern Virginia?

Prices vary by company and job. Factors include crew size, inventory, distance, stairs, elevators, packing, and truck requirements.

Request an inventory-based estimate instead of assuming a regional average applies to your move.

8. Do movers charge extra for stairs?

Policies vary.

Some companies charge separate stair fees, while others account for stairs through labor time.

Ask about the policy for both addresses.

9. Are moving trucks included in the quote?

Usually in a crew-and-truck package, but not in labor-only service.

Confirm truck use, fuel, mileage, and transportation charges in writing.

10. Do moving companies provide packing services?

Many offer full or partial packing as an additional service.

Confirm whether packing labor and supplies are included in the estimate.

11. Can movers disassemble and reassemble beds?

Many companies offer basic furniture disassembly and reassembly, but the scope varies.

Identify the furniture requiring this service before booking.

12. How long does a local move take?

A smaller apartment may take a few hours, while a larger home can require most of a day or longer.

Inventory, crew size, access, packing, and travel determine the actual schedule.

13. Should I hire two, three, or four movers?

Ask the estimator to recommend a crew based on your actual inventory and property access.

A larger crew may reduce duration, but the total cost depends on the time saved and the applicable rates.

14. Do moving companies offer senior or military discounts?

Some do.

Ask each company about eligibility, applicable services, and whether discounts can be combined.

15. Can movers handle a same-day or last-minute move?

Some companies offer short-notice scheduling, but availability is not guaranteed.

Provide the date, inventory, and both addresses as soon as possible.

16. Can I hire movers just to load a rental truck or POD?

Yes, some companies offer labor-only loading and unloading.

Confirm whether equipment, furniture protection, and any minimum hours are included.

17. Are apartment moves different from house moves?

They can be.

Apartment moves may require elevators, loading docks, and building reservations. House moves may involve larger inventories, garages, and multiple floors.

18. What should I do if a mover damages my furniture?

Document the issue with photographs, review the applicable contract and liability terms, and submit a claim through the company's stated process.

For Virginia household-goods carriers, DMV publishes specific requirements for handling written loss and damage claims.

19. Is a moving deposit normal?

Some companies require deposits, but policies differ.

Read the refund and cancellation terms and verify the company before paying.

20. Can I use a Northern Virginia mover to move into Maryland or DC?

Some companies provide interstate or cross-jurisdictional service, but you should verify the authority applicable to the route.

A short drive across a state or district boundary should not automatically be treated as a Virginia-only move.

21. What is the difference between a moving company and a moving broker?

A moving carrier transports household goods. A broker arranges transportation with a carrier.

FMCSA advises interstate customers to understand which type of business they're hiring and identify the company that will perform the move.

22. How far in advance should I book movers?

Book as soon as your moving date is reasonably certain, especially if you have a fixed elevator reservation or a large household.

Lead times vary by company, season, and availability.

23. Should I trust online moving-company rankings?

Use rankings as a starting point, not your only evidence.

Check the publisher's methodology, sponsorship disclosures, review dates, and whether the information matches current official records.

24. How can I compare movers if their quotes are very different?

Ask each company to explain its assumptions, included services, crew size, expected duration, and possible additional charges.

An unusually low quote may reflect a different scope rather than the same job at a lower price.

25. What is the most important thing to verify before hiring movers?

Confirm that the company can legally perform your specific move and that you understand the written estimate and service agreement.

Reviews, price, and availability are useful, but they do not replace regulatory verification.
