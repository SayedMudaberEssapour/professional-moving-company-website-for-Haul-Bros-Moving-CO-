Sterling sits in eastern Loudoun County, right next to Washington Dulles International Airport, and it has a bit of everything: older townhome neighborhoods, planned communities near Route 7, apartments and condos around Dulles Town Center, and homes near the Potomac River. That variety is exactly why moves here can go smoothly or sideways. This guide comes from the perspective of a moving company. Quick answer: what should you know before moving to Sterling, VA?

Sterling is in Loudoun County, and Loudoun's rules apply. Vehicles must be declared with the Commissioner of the Revenue within 60 days of moving a vehicle into the county, and the county doesn't provide trash or recycling collection.

Your building or HOA usually controls move day. Ask about truck parking, elevator reservations, move hours, and insurance certificates before you book.

Airport noise varies by address. Loudoun's Airport Impact Overlay District covers properties near Dulles, and disclosure requirements depend on the property and current rules.

What makes a Sterling move different

1. Housing type decides the logistics

Sterling is less one neighborhood than a collection of them, and the type of home you're moving into matters more than the zip code:

Single-family homes usually have a driveway, so trucks can load and unload close to the door.

Townhomes often share narrow lanes, limited guest parking, and multi-level stairs. Ask your HOA how many spaces a truck may occupy and for how long.

Apartments and condos, including those near Dulles Town Center, commonly require elevator reservations, loading dock coordination, and sometimes a certificate of insurance from your mover.

Newer communities may have construction traffic and HOA move-in hours.

Well-known areas people search include Sterling Park, Cascades, Potomac Falls, Lowes Island, Countryside, Sugarland Run, and the Dulles Town Center area. Loudoun County's residential community maps can help you see how an address fits into its subdivision.

2. Your HOA may have rules for moving day

Many Sterling homes belong to homeowners or condo associations. Before moving week, ask for the association's rules on:

Where a moving truck can legally park, and whether approval is needed

Move-in hours and days

Elevator or loading-dock reservations and gate codes

Guest-parking limits and commercial-vehicle rules

Move-in fees or deposits

3. Routes matter near Dulles

Sterling is near Route 7, Route 28, the Dulles Toll Road, and the airport, so timing is important. Rush-hour traffic can add hours to an hourly move. Haul Bros plans start times and routes to keep you off the busiest stretches when possible. Confirm a legal truck route with your mover; truck restrictions, clearances, and permitted access may differ from passenger-car routes.

4. Airport noise is address-specific

Loudoun County's Airport Impact Overlay District is a zoning designation for properties where airport noise is anticipated at specified levels. Review the current county map and disclosure guidance for your address; some notification requirements have changed. Before you commit to a home in Sterling, check the county's map and visit at different times of day.

Getting around Sterling

Loudoun County Transit launched Transit On Demand in Sterling on July 1, 2026, as a fare-free pilot in a 14-square-mile area with more than 300 stops. It connects to other local Loudoun County bus routes and runs weekdays from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. and weekends from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. Loudoun's Silver Line stations, including Loudoun Gateway, are also nearby. Since this is a pilot, check the county's Transit On Demand page for current service details.
