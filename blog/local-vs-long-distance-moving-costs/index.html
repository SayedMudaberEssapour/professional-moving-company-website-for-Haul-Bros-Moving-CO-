Local vs. Long-Distance Moving Costs: What You'll Actually Pay in 2026

Quick Answer: Local moves are billed hourly and typically cost $395–$900+ depending on crew size and time, while long-distance moves are billed by shipment weight and mileage, typically running $2,300 for an in-town 2–3 bedroom move versus roughly $4,300–$5,500+ for the same home moved 1,000+ miles. The real difference isn't just distance — it's the entire pricing model.

If you're comparing local and long-distance moving costs, you're really comparing two different pricing systems. Local moves are priced by the hour. Long-distance moves are priced by weight and mileage, following federal tariff rules that apply to interstate carriers. Understanding which system applies to your move — and what drives the price within it — is the fastest way to budget accurately and avoid a surprise number on moving day.

This guide breaks down exactly how each pricing model works, what a real move costs at each distance, and how to know which one applies to you.

Neither model is "more expensive" by default — a large local move with four movers for six hours can cost more than a small long-distance move of a few hundred miles. Distance alone doesn't determine your total; volume and time do.

How Local Moving Costs Work

Local moves — generally under 50–100 miles within the same metro area — are billed hourly. You're paying for the crew's time and the truck, not for miles traveled. That's why a two-bedroom apartment move across town can cost less than moving the same apartment two states over, even though the truck barely goes anywhere.

What increases a local moving bill:

  • Larger crew needed for heavier inventory or tight timelines
  • Stairs, elevators, or long carries from the truck to your door
  • Additional stops (storage unit, second location)
  • Packing services added to the move
  • Specialty items requiring extra care or disassembly

How Long-Distance Moving Costs Work

Long-distance and interstate moves work on a completely different system. Licensed carriers under FMCSA regulation price by a tariff: your shipment's certified weight, multiplied by a rate tied to the mileage band between your origin and destination. The truck is weighed empty before loading and again after loading — the difference is your billable weight.

The two numbers that matter most:

1. Weight — More items means a heavier shipment and a higher weight-based charge. A typical 2-bedroom home runs 5,000–7,000 lbs.

2. Distance — Longer routes add mileage and fuel costs, though the *rate per mile* actually tends to drop slightly on longer hauls even as the total goes up.

Typical national averages for 2026:

  • In-town move of a 2–3 bedroom home (about 7,400 lbs): roughly **$2,300**
  • Same home moved 1,000+ miles: roughly **$4,300–$5,500**
  • Full-service long-distance moves overall: commonly range from **$2,500 to $8,000+**, depending on home size and distance

What increases a long-distance moving bill:

  • Total shipment weight
  • Total mileage between pickup and delivery
  • Full packing vs. self-packing
  • Peak season (May–September) and month-end dates
  • Guaranteed/expedited delivery windows
  • Long carries, stairs, or elevators at either address

Local vs. Long-Distance: Which Actually Costs More For You?

Run through these three questions:

How far are you moving? Under roughly 50–100 miles within the same state, you're almost always in local, hourly-priced territory. Beyond that — especially across state lines — you're in weight-and-mileage territory.

How much are you moving? A heavily loaded local move (large home, four movers, six-plus hours) can easily cost $1,500–$2,500 — in the same range as a *short* long-distance move. Volume matters as much as distance.

Do you need packing done for you? Full packing adds cost to either pricing model, but it has a bigger dollar impact on long-distance moves since packed boxes add to your billable weight.

Ways to Lower Your Moving Cost

  • **Move mid-week and mid-month** — weekends, month-end dates, and summer carry a price premium in both models
  • **Downsize before you move** — fewer items means fewer hours (local) or less billable weight (long-distance)
  • **Pack yourself** where possible — self-packing saves labor time locally and reduces shipment weight long-distance
  • **Book 4–6 weeks ahead** — last-minute bookings limit your crew and date options
  • **Get a written estimate, not a phone quote** — this is the single biggest way to avoid a number that grows on moving day

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to move locally or long-distance?

Local moves are cheaper in total dollars simply because they involve less time and no mileage-based tariff. But cost-per-mile actually favors longer moves — it's total volume and distance together that set your final price, not distance alone.

What counts as a "local" move vs. a "long-distance" move?

Most companies define local as under 50–100 miles within the same state, billed hourly. Long-distance typically means 100+ miles, often crossing state lines, billed by weight and regulated under federal tariff rules.

How much does a local move cost with Haul Bros?

How much does a long-distance move cost in 2026?

National averages run about $2,300 for an in-town 2–3 bedroom move and $4,300–$5,500+ for the same home moved 1,000 miles, with full-service long-distance moves commonly landing between $2,500 and $8,000+ depending on size and distance.

Why do long-distance movers charge by weight instead of by the hour?

Federal regulations require FMCSA-licensed interstate carriers to price by certified shipment weight and mileage rather than by time, since a long-haul truck's costs are driven by what it carries and how far it travels — not how many hours a local crew works.

Does packing cost more for local or long-distance moves?

Both cost more with packing added, but long-distance moves feel it more directly — every packed box adds to your billable shipment weight, while local moves only add packing labor time.
