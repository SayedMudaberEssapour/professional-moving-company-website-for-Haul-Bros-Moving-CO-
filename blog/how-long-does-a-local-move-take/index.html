How Long Does a Local Move Take? A Complete Moving Day Guide

By Haul Bros Moving Co LLC · September 23, 2026

Wondering how long your local move will take? Discover realistic moving timelines for studios, apartments, townhouses, and houses. Learn how crew size, stairs, elevators, packing, and traffic affect moving day, plus practical tips for a smoother move in Woodbridge, Fairfax, Alexandria, Arlington, and throughout Northern Virginia.

Planning a local move in Northern Virginia? One of the first questions you probably have is: How long will the movers take?

You might be moving from a one-bedroom apartment in Woodbridge to a condo in Alexandria, relocating from a Fairfax townhouse to a house in Burke, or moving your family across Manassas.

You need to know how much time to reserve, whether you'll finish before evening, how long to book your freight elevator, and what your moving bill might look like.

The answer depends on more than the number of bedrooms.

The amount of furniture, number of boxes, crew size, stairs, parking, preparation, and driving distance can all change the schedule.

This guide breaks down realistic local moving times by home size, explains what happens during each stage of moving day, and shows how to prepare for a more efficient relocation.

Quick answer: How long does a local move usually take?

A typical local move can take approximately 3–8 hours for many smaller and medium-sized households, while larger homes may require 8–12 hours or more.

A lightly furnished studio may finish sooner. A large house with extensive belongings, difficult access, or packing services may take longer or require multiple days.

These are broad planning estimates, not guaranteed completion times. Published moving-company guides report varying ranges based on crew size, inventory, and their assumptions.

1. What counts as a local move?

In everyday language, a local move generally means relocating within the same city or metropolitan area.

For example, a move from Woodbridge to Lake Ridge or Fairfax to Burke would commonly be described as local.

However, “local” is not a universal legal or pricing category. Virginia intrastate operating-authority rules and a moving company's own pricing structure may distinguish moves by distance and other factors.

A move from Virginia into Washington, DC, or Maryland crosses a jurisdictional boundary and should not automatically be treated as an ordinary Virginia intrastate move, even when the driving distance is short.

For this article, local moving refers to a relatively short-distance residential relocation where pickup, transportation, and delivery are planned as one moving operation.

The examples below focus on the total time from the crew's arrival through completion of unloading. Confirm your mover's actual billing policy separately.

2. How long does a studio apartment move take?

Studio apartment

2–4 hours

Suggested crew: 2 movers

A studio generally contains fewer large furniture pieces than a larger home, but the actual inventory still matters.

A studio move might include a bed, dresser, sofa, television, desk, kitchen items, and boxes.

A relatively simple studio move with ground-floor access and a short drive may finish near the lower end of the range.

It may take longer when the apartment has multiple flights of stairs, a long hallway, restricted elevator access, or a large amount of furniture.

Example: A lightly furnished studio moving between two ground-floor apartments in Woodbridge may require considerably less time than a densely furnished studio moving from an upper floor in Arlington to another high-rise.

Planning tip: Have every box sealed and labeled before the crew arrives.

3. How long does a one-bedroom apartment move take?

One-bedroom apartment

3–5 hours

Suggested crew: 2 movers

A one-bedroom move typically involves bedroom furniture, living room furniture, kitchen belongings, and packed boxes.

The crew may need to protect furniture, disassemble a bed frame, load the truck, travel to the destination, unload, and reassemble the bed.

A one-bedroom move may take longer if the customer has a large sectional, oversized furniture, extensive boxes, or difficult building access.

For apartment moves, elevator availability and the distance from the apartment to the truck can matter as much as the driving distance.

Northern Virginia example: A one-bedroom move from an Alexandria high-rise to another apartment nearby could still take several hours if both buildings require elevator coordination and long walks to the loading areas.

4. How long does a two-bedroom apartment move take?

Two-bedroom apartment

4–6 hours

Suggested crew: 2–3 movers

Two-bedroom apartments often include additional beds, dressers, desks, living room furniture, and more boxes.

A three-person crew may be appropriate when the inventory is substantial, the building has stairs, or there is a long distance between the apartment and truck.

However, adding another mover doesn't automatically reduce the total price. The estimate should consider both the larger crew's rate and the expected time savings.

Planning tip: Tell your moving company whether your second bedroom is a lightly furnished guest room, a fully furnished bedroom, or an office filled with desks and equipment.

Those are very different inventories.

5. How long does a townhouse move take?

Two- or three-level townhouse

4–8+ hours

Suggested crew: Often 3–4 movers, depending on inventory

Townhouses frequently involve multiple floors, staircases, and furniture that must be maneuvered through tight turns.

A townhouse in Woodbridge, Dale City, Manassas, or Centreville may have bedrooms upstairs, a living area on the main floor, and additional belongings in a basement or garage.

The number of bedrooms alone won't capture that complexity.

A lightly furnished two-bedroom townhouse might take approximately four to six hours with a suitable crew.

A three-bedroom townhouse with a finished basement, extensive furniture, and garage contents may require substantially more time.

Planning tip: Include the basement, garage, attic, patio, and storage areas in your inventory.

6. How long does a three-bedroom house move take?

Three-bedroom house

5–8 hours

Suggested crew: 3–4 movers

A three-bedroom home usually requires more coordination because of the larger furniture inventory and greater number of boxes.

The moving team may need to handle multiple bed frames, dressers, sofas, dining furniture, desks, outdoor items, and garage belongings.

A three-bedroom move can fit within a standard workday when the inventory and access are manageable.

However, a fully furnished home with a basement, garage, and extensive packing needs may exceed the estimate.

A suitable crew size can help distribute tasks efficiently, but the company should evaluate the actual inventory before recommending the number of movers.

Planning tip: Schedule packing separately when practical if you have a large household and want to reduce the amount of work remaining on moving day.

7. How long does a four-bedroom house move take?

Four-bedroom house

8–12+ hours

Suggested crew: Often 4 movers or a larger custom arrangement

Four-bedroom homes can involve significant furniture, boxes, storage areas, and specialty items.

The moving company should evaluate whether the shipment fits in the planned truck and whether one moving day is realistic.

A 26-foot moving truck is not guaranteed to hold every four-bedroom household. Capacity depends on the volume and shape of the belongings, not just the number of bedrooms.

Some moves may require additional truck capacity, multiple trips, a larger crew, or a two-day plan.

Planning tip: Request a detailed inventory review before scheduling a large move.

8. How long does a five-bedroom or larger move take?

A five-bedroom home may require 10–14 hours or more, depending on inventory, access, crew size, and transportation arrangements.

Some large moves are better divided into separate packing and moving days.

A large home may contain multiple living rooms, office furniture, a finished basement, garage equipment, outdoor furniture, and substantial storage.

Don't assume the move can be completed in one day without a detailed assessment.

For larger households, ask whether the company recommends a physical or virtual survey, additional truck capacity, or a staged moving schedule.

Does loading or unloading take longer?

Loading often involves additional preparation, including furniture protection, disassembly, and securing items inside the truck.

Unloading may be faster for a straightforward move, but that is not guaranteed.

A destination with three flights of stairs, a distant loading area, or complex furniture placement can make unloading take longer than expected.

The relevant question is not simply how long the truck takes to load. It is how long the entire agreed job will take.

10. The 12 biggest factors that affect moving time

1. The amount of furniture and boxes

A two-bedroom apartment with minimal furniture may be faster to move than a one-bedroom apartment packed with books, exercise equipment, and oversized furniture.

Inventory volume is more useful than bedroom count alone.

Tell your estimator about closets, garages, storage units, patios, and other spaces.

2. Crew size

A properly sized crew can distribute work and reduce bottlenecks.

However, the relationship between crew size and duration is not perfectly proportional.

Four movers will not necessarily complete every job in half the time of two movers.

The home layout, truck access, and ability to work simultaneously matter.

3. Packing readiness

If boxes aren't packed when the crew arrives, the move may take longer.

Loose kitchen items, clothing, decorations, and small electronics can create significant delays.

If you need packing assistance, arrange it in advance rather than assuming it is included.

4. Stairs

Carrying furniture down three flights of stairs takes more work than moving it through a ground-floor entrance.

Narrow staircases and difficult turns may also require extra preparation.

Disclose the number of flights at both addresses.

5. Elevator access

An elevator can make a high-floor move manageable, but it may also create a bottleneck.

Shared elevators, long waits, restricted hours, and distant loading docks can increase the time required.

Reserve a freight elevator when your building requires it.

6. Distance between the truck and your door

A long carry can add time to every trip.

A truck parked directly outside a townhouse creates a different situation from a truck parked across a large apartment complex.

Tell the mover where the truck can park and how far the crew must walk.

7. Driving distance and traffic

A move within the same neighborhood may involve a short drive.

A move from Woodbridge to Alexandria, Fairfax to Reston, or Stafford to Manassas may require more travel time depending on the route and traffic.

Northern Virginia congestion can affect the schedule, particularly when a move involves major commuter corridors.

The exact time depends on the date, departure time, and current conditions.

8. Furniture disassembly and reassembly

Beds, dining tables, sectional sofas, and other large items may need to be disassembled.

Reassembly at the destination also takes time.

Identify the furniture that needs this service before the estimate.

9. Specialty items

Pianos, safes, large mirrors, treadmills, oversized refrigerators, and other specialty items may require special equipment or handling.

Some items may not be suitable for the planned crew or truck.

Provide photographs, dimensions, and approximate weights.

10. Truck capacity

If the entire shipment doesn't fit in the truck, an additional trip or more capacity may be necessary.

This can significantly affect the timeline.

A complete inventory helps the moving company determine whether the planned truck is suitable.

11. Weather

Rain, snow, ice, and extreme heat can affect loading, unloading, driving, and safe working conditions.

The team may need additional time to protect floors and belongings or adjust the schedule.

Avoid relying on an exact finish time when severe weather is possible.

12. Unexpected access problems

Locked loading docks, missing gate codes, unavailable elevators, parking conflicts, and delayed access to the new home can interrupt the move.

Confirm access arrangements with both properties before moving day.

13. Does packing count toward moving time?

It depends on your agreement.

If you hire movers for loading, transportation, and unloading only, packing your boxes is generally your responsibility unless the company agrees otherwise.

If you purchase packing services, the time required should be included in the service plan.

Packing a kitchen, fragile artwork, books, clothing, and electronics can take substantial additional time.

For larger households, packing may be scheduled on a separate day.

Before booking, ask: “Does your estimated moving time include packing, or should everything be boxed before the crew arrives?”

That single question can prevent a major scheduling misunderstanding.

14. When does the moving clock start and stop?

There is no universal answer that applies to every moving company and every contract.

Some movers begin billing when the crew arrives. Others have travel-time provisions or different minimums.

For Haul Bros' established local service, the stated policy is:

Moving time starts when the team arrives and ends after unloading is complete, with a two-hour minimum.

Customers should confirm that the current written estimate and applicable tariff reflect this policy.

Ask your mover whether the following are included in billable time:

- The initial walkthrough and furniture preparation.

- Loading and transportation.

- Unloading and agreed furniture reassembly.

- Any travel to or from the company's facility.

- Waiting time caused by unavailable access.

Get the billing method in writing before booking.

For interstate moves, federal rules governing written estimates and charges are different from ordinary local-move pricing. FMCSA advises consumers to review written estimates and understand the services and charges included.

15. How to make your local move faster without sacrificing care

The goal should be an efficient, organized move—not rushing heavy furniture or skipping protection.

Here are practical ways to reduce avoidable delays.

Finish packing before arrival. Seal boxes, label them, and keep them in accessible locations.

Create a complete inventory. Include the basement, garage, storage unit, balcony, and patio.

Separate items that aren't moving. Clearly mark or relocate belongings that should remain behind.

Reserve parking and elevators. Confirm the arrangements at both addresses.

Measure difficult furniture. Check large pieces against doorways, staircases, and elevator dimensions.

Arrange child and pet care. Keep pathways clear and reduce interruptions.

Prepare the new home. Make sure you have keys, gate codes, electricity, and access.

Label destination rooms. Clear labels reduce confusion during unloading.

Keep essentials with you. Carry medication, identification, keys, valuables, and important documents personally.

Be available for decisions. Have someone authorized to answer questions during loading and unloading.

FMCSA's moving guidance similarly recommends supervising the loading and unloading process, reviewing paperwork, and ensuring that the shipment inventory is accurate for interstate moves.

16. How much extra time should you reserve?

Your moving estimate is a planning tool, not an exact appointment for when the job will end.

For a straightforward small move, a modest scheduling buffer may be sufficient.

For a large home, a high-rise apartment, difficult access, or a move involving substantial traffic, reserve more flexibility.

Rather than applying a universal percentage, ask the estimator:

“What is your expected duration, what could extend it, and how much time should I reserve for the elevator or loading dock?”

If your building allows only a strict moving window, disclose it before booking so the company can determine whether the job can realistically fit.

Avoid scheduling a home closing, flight, utility shutoff, or another immovable appointment immediately after the estimated finish time.

Frequently Asked Questions About Local Moving Times

These questions address common practical customer concerns. They are not presented as a verified ranking of Google search queries.

1. How many hours does a typical local move take?

Many smaller and medium-sized local moves can take approximately three to eight hours. Larger homes may require eight to twelve hours or more.

The actual duration depends on inventory, crew size, packing, stairs, elevators, parking, and travel.

2. Can movers complete a local move in two hours?

Some small, lightly furnished moves can be completed in two hours when everything is prepared and access is straightforward.

A two-hour minimum does not mean every move will finish within two hours.

3. How long does it take two movers to move a one-bedroom apartment?

A reasonable planning range is approximately three to five hours for a prepared one-bedroom apartment with a short local drive and manageable access.

Difficult stairs, elevators, extensive boxes, or large furniture may extend the schedule.

4. How long does a two-bedroom apartment move take?

A prepared two-bedroom apartment may take approximately four to six hours with a suitable crew.

The estimate may be longer for high-rise buildings, difficult access, or extensive belongings.

5. How long does it take to move a three-bedroom townhouse?

A three-bedroom townhouse may take approximately five to eight hours or more, depending on its inventory, number of floors, crew size, and transportation requirements.

Include basement and garage contents in your inventory.

6. How long does it take four movers to move a house?

It depends on the house and shipment.

A suitably staffed three-bedroom house might take approximately five to eight hours. A fully furnished four-bedroom house may require eight to twelve hours or more.

Four movers do not guarantee a particular completion time.

7. How long does loading a 26-foot moving truck take?

Loading time depends on the shipment volume, crew size, furniture preparation, stairs, and distance from the home to the truck.

For a substantial household shipment, loading alone may take several hours.

A 26-foot truck is not guaranteed to hold every large household. Request an inventory assessment.

8. Is unloading faster than loading?

It can be because furniture protection and securing the truck are generally handled during loading.

However, unloading may take longer if the destination has stairs, difficult access, or extensive furniture-placement requirements.

9. Do stairs make moving take longer?

Usually, stairs increase the amount of physical handling required.

The actual effect depends on the number of flights, staircase width, furniture dimensions, crew size, and access.

10. Does an elevator make a move faster?

Not necessarily.

A reserved freight elevator may improve coordination, but long hallways, elevator wait times, and restricted access can still slow the move.

11. How long should I reserve a freight elevator?

Ask your moving company for an estimate based on your inventory and building access.

Confirm whether the building requires separate reservations for loading and unloading.

Avoid booking an elevator window shorter than the time reasonably needed at that property.

12. Does driving time count toward the moving bill?

It depends on the company's billing policy and applicable terms.

For Haul Bros' stated local service, time begins on arrival and ends after unloading, so the drive between properties is part of the move.

Confirm the current written terms before booking.

13. How much longer does moving from Woodbridge to Alexandria take than moving within Woodbridge?

The difference depends on the exact addresses, departure time, traffic, and access at both properties.

A longer drive can add time, but an apartment with difficult loading access may have a greater effect than the driving distance.

14. Can I move a two-bedroom apartment in four hours?

It may be possible with a suitable crew, prepared belongings, a short drive, and straightforward access.

Four hours should not be treated as a guarantee without an inventory review.

15. Can movers pack and move my home in one day?

Some smaller homes may be suitable for same-day packing and moving.

Larger households or extensive fragile-item packing may require a separate packing day.

Discuss the scope before scheduling.

16. Does furniture disassembly count toward moving time?

It generally requires labor and should be accounted for in the estimate.

Ask which items the movers will disassemble and reassemble and whether those services are included.

17. What happens if my move takes longer than estimated?

The answer depends on the pricing arrangement and written agreement.

For an hourly move, additional billable time may increase the final charge according to the agreed rate and billing increments.

Review those terms before booking.

18. What if the movers finish earlier than estimated?

Your final charge depends on the contract, minimum hours, and pricing method.

For an hourly move, ask whether you pay for actual billable time subject to the minimum or a fixed agreed amount.

19. What is the best time to start a local move?

An early start can leave more of the day available for loading, travel, and unloading.

However, the most appropriate arrival time depends on your building's moving hours, traffic, closing schedule, and crew availability.

20. Should I take the entire day off for a local move?

It is sensible to reserve the full day when possible, particularly for a two-bedroom or larger move.

Even a shorter estimate can be affected by traffic, building access, weather, or inventory changes.

21. How can I make movers work faster?

Prepare your home, pack boxes, label rooms, clear pathways, arrange parking and elevator access, and provide a complete inventory.

Avoid pressuring the crew to rush or skip safe handling and furniture protection.

22. How much time should I allow for a move with a baby or pets?

The physical moving estimate depends on the inventory and property, but arranging separate childcare or pet care can reduce interruptions and keep pathways safer.

Keep essential supplies accessible.

23. Do I need to be present during the entire move?

You or an authorized representative should be available to answer questions, confirm instructions, and review the completed work and paperwork.

If you cannot be present, arrange a responsible representative in advance.

24. How do I get the most accurate moving-time estimate?

Provide a complete inventory, both addresses, floor numbers, stairs, elevator information, parking details, and any specialty items.

A virtual or in-person survey may help for larger or more complicated moves.

25. Can Haul Bros tell me exactly when my move will finish?

Haul Bros can prepare an estimated duration based on the information you provide.

The final completion time cannot be guaranteed solely from the number of bedrooms because inventory, access, traffic, and other conditions may change.
